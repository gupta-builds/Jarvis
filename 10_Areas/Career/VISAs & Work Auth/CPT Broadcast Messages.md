# CPT Broadcast Messages
Two unsigned emails to school officials in August 2026 are the entire reason my school stopped approving CPT. This note captures both in full, section by section, so I never have to rely on someone else's summary of them. Read it before arguing about what the government "announced," and again after any court ruling to see which sentences survived.
## In Plain Words
A **broadcast message** is an email that a government office sends to every school official in the country. These two went to the people at every college who sign international students' papers. They said, in effect: from now on, only approve CPT if the training is required for every student in the degree, and if you approve anything else, your school could lose its right to enroll international students and you could face penalties for false statements. The government also called them "reminders," as if nothing had changed, which is the heart of the lawsuit. At UMN, the result is that ISSS stopped approving elective CPT ([[ISSS October Meeting Brief]], [[US Immigration Rules Explained]]).
## What They Are
==The two messages never admit the rule changed; they restate "integral part of an established curriculum" as "required of all students" and attach perjury and decertification risk to the older reading.== Both are addressed "To: PDSOs and DSOs at SEVP-certified schools." BCM 2608-01 (August 12, 2026) is titled "Reminder of Liability for DSOs Regarding CPT Authorization." BCM 2608-02 (August 24, 2026) is titled "Guidance for Designated School Officials regarding Curricular Practical Training." Each ends with the same disclaimer that it is not "itself a rule or a final action by SEVP" and creates no enforceable right ([[SEVP and DSOs]]).
The regulation they interpret did not change. It still reads that CPT is "an integral part of an established curriculum" and is defined as "alternative work/study, internship, cooperative education or any other type of required internship or practicum that is offered by sponsoring employers through cooperative agreements with the school" ([[CPT Program]]).
## BCM 2608-01, August 12
SEVP says it "has observed a rise in CPT authorizations that do not meet regulatory requirements, specifically those not constituting an integral part of an established curriculum," and is "increasing its scrutiny of CPT approvals."
1. **CPT must be integral to the curriculum**
	DSOs may authorize CPT only when the training is an integral part of an established curriculum and directly related to the major. It "cannot be authorized solely for the purpose of employment or for experiences that are not required or otherwise formally recognized as part of the academic program." SEVP "reserves the right to request documentation and other evidence from DSOs to identify whether CPT is indeed a core and essential part of the student's studies, without which, they would be unable to complete their degree."
	The same paragraph keeps one opening for schools: "SEVP recognizes that the school, not the U.S. government, defines what is and is not required to complete a degree." It then adds that SEVP is "committed to identifying and investigating schools that attempt to undermine or purposely bypass federal regulations or oversight."
2. **Abuse and escalation**
	Students attest under penalty of perjury that they are in the country "solely for the purpose of pursuing a full program of study." If SEVP suspects officials of "abusing CPT to create work opportunities rather than academic studies," they face scrutiny from SEVP and Homeland Security Investigations, "to include potential withdrawal of the school's SEVP certification."
3. **DSO certification and legal responsibility**
	Signing or re-signing the I-20 to authorize CPT certifies under penalty of perjury that the information is true and correct, "including confirming that CPT is required or otherwise integral to the student's curriculum."
4. **Potential liability**
	"Improper CPT authorization, such as for non-integral purposes, may subject both the institution and individual DSO to administrative or legal consequences. This includes withdrawal of SEVP certification and potential penalties for providing false information or misrepresenting the nature of the training."
5. **Actions requested**
	Review all CPT requests and the school's academic requirements "across all fields of study." Authorize CPT "only if its absence would make the attainment of a degree an impossibility and if the work associated with CPT is required for all students pursuing that degree." Keep thorough records of the curricular necessity of each authorization. Consult the SEVP field representative on specific cases.
The message closes by inviting employers, DSOs, students and the public to report suspected fraud to SEVPFraud@hsi.dhs.gov and gives the SEVP Response Center contacts.
## BCM 2608-02, August 24
This message restates the August 12 message, says SEVP's audits found schools describing CPT "as if it is optional practical training," and warns that "failure to comply with SEVP regulations may result in an institution losing certification to enroll foreign students." It then answers seven questions.
1. **How is CPT defined in regulation?** It quotes the regulatory definition above.
2. **How is CPT different from OPT?** OPT "is not intended to fulfill requirements of a student's program of study and successful program completion does not require participation in OPT; it is optional." CPT "fulfills an integral element of an established curriculum and successful completion is contingent on participation in CPT." It defines "integral" by dictionary as "essential to completeness."
3. **Can CPT be part of an elective course?** No: "In any instance where an elective course is optional, making the CPT optional, the integral and required aspects of CPT would not be met." The training must be required of all participating students "regardless of citizenship."
4. **Must all students in the curriculum participate?** Yes. "All students -- foreign and American -- are required to participate in practical training if they are enrolled in an established curriculum where practical training is integral." SEVP says it found schools that had not required everyone in the same curriculum to participate.
5. **Must students request authorization from the DSO?** Yes, so the school can confirm the curriculum, the relation to the major, and update SEVIS. A DSO who fails to maintain this verification "may face significant SEVP sanctions," citing 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(i), 214.3(l)(2), and 214.4(a)(2).
6. **Must all CPT use employers with cooperative agreements?** Yes, with "legitimate, bona fide employers," and "Students are not expected to obtain EADs for this training."
7. **What if a DSO is pressured by school leadership?** A new confidential SEVP tipline takes those reports for review by SEVP and Homeland Security Investigations.
## What The Language Does And Does Not Say
- *It does say:* training must be required of every student in the curriculum, an optional elective fails, and the school decides what is required. The last point matters, because it means a school can create a program where the training is required of everyone enrolled. Question 4 speaks of "an established curriculum that the school has determined requires CPT."
- *It does not say* anything about students already authorized, about whether it applies to past authorizations, or about the for-credit pathway the plaintiffs say the 1991 rule created ([[CPT Lawsuit]]). Silence is not retroactivity, but the plaintiffs say DHS is already asking about earlier CPT.
- *It contains a tension.* Message 1 says CPT cannot be used for experiences "not required or otherwise formally recognized as part of the academic program," which could cover recognized-but-elective training. Its action item then says only "if its absence would make the attainment of a degree an impossibility." Message 2 resolves the tension in the stricter direction.
## How Schools Reacted
Sources here are the plaintiffs' own filings, so treat them as claims until a court weighs them. The complaint says these schools narrowed CPT: Boston University, Clark, Columbia, Cornell, Dartmouth, Foothill-De Anza, Grinnell, Michigan State, Mount Holyoke, Illinois Urbana-Champaign, Penn, Suffolk, Tufts, Wisconsin, Vanderbilt, and Washington University in St. Louis.
The supporting declarations report that Columbia denied Fall CPT to more than 120 international students, Grinnell had at least 13 students lose Fall 2026 internships in finance, consulting and information technology, and Cornell said many programs were considering making experiences mandatory for everyone. Yale's international office says it has processed CPT only for training required of all students since August 24. UMN's ISSS pages, last updated September 3, say it is pausing all CPT applications ([[CPT Program]]).
## Reading These As A Student
- The messages are addressed to DSOs, so a student cannot "violate" one. The consequence reaches a student as a denied or withdrawn authorization.
- Keep the course syllabus, credit hours, and the original I-20 for any past CPT. The plaintiffs allege DHS officers have started asking about earlier CPT when students apply for other benefits.
- The path that survives the messages is a curriculum where everyone does the training. If a CS program ever adopts one, CPT returns for that program only.
## Words Used In This Note
- *Perjury:* lying while swearing something is true. DSOs sign I-20s "under penalty of perjury."
- *Certification (of a school):* SEVP's approval that lets a school enroll F-1 students. Losing it is the worst outcome for a school.
- *Cooperative agreement:* a formal arrangement between a school and an employer for student training.
- *Tipline:* a confidential channel for reporting concerns to SEVP.
