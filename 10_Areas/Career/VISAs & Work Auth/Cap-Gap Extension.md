# Cap-Gap Extension
The cap-gap rule is the bridge between the day OPT work authorization ends and the day H-1B work authorization begins, and without it a selected H-1B worker could be forced to stop working for months. This note captures USCIS's rules for it, the way it can fail, and how it interacts with the other 2026 changes. Read it before an employer files a cap-subject H-1B petition for me.
## Why The Gap Exists
==An employer cannot file a cap-subject H-1B petition more than six months before the work start, so the earliest filing is April 1 and the earliest start is October 1, which leaves five months in which an OPT permit may already have expired.== USCIS describes it this way: the cap-gap period starts when the student's status and employment authorization expire and, unless terminated, ends on April 1 of the fiscal year for which H-1B status is requested or at the validity start date of the approved petition, whichever is earlier.
For a May 2028 graduate with an H-1B for fiscal year 2029, the petition would be filed from April 1, 2028, the start date would be October 1, 2028, and the cap-gap ceiling would be April 1, 2029 ([[2028 Graduation Timeline]]).
## Who Qualifies
- The petition is cap-subject and requests a **change of status** to H-1B, not consular processing. It is timely filed during the H-1B filing period, beginning April 1, while the student's F-1 stay is still valid, including OPT and the 60-day period after.
- It is based on a valid, selected registration for the same beneficiary and fiscal year, requests a start in the fiscal year, and is nonfrivolous ([[H-1B Lottery]]).
- The student has not violated status.
- A petition for a cap-exempt employer such as a university does not qualify, because cap-gap applies only to cap-subject petitions ([[H1B Visa]]).
## What It Does
The extension is automatic. The student does not file anything or receive a new EAD. The only proof is an updated I-20 from the DSO showing the extension, which the student may request by bringing the petition and its mailing receipt, and later the I-797 receipt notice. USCIS says the student can keep working while that I-20 is processed, because the I-20 merely evidences the extension.
It also extends the dependents' stay. If the petition is approved, the extension runs to April 1 or the approved validity start date, whichever is earlier. If the petition is denied, rejected, revoked, or withdrawn, or the change-of-status request is denied or withdrawn, the extension ends, even if the petition is approved for consular processing.
## How It Fails
1. **A denied or withdrawn petition**
	The student gets the standard 60-day period to leave, counted from the notification date. There is no 60-day period and no cap-gap if the denial rests on a status violation, misrepresentation or fraud.
2. **Starting the gap in the grace period**
	A student already in the 60-day period when the petition is filed gets the F-1 extension but is not authorized to work, because work authorization had ended when the petition was filed.
3. **Travel during the gap**
	An F-1 student may travel and return in F-1 status only if the petition and change of status are already approved and the student seeks readmission before the change takes effect. Leaving while the petition and change of status are pending is treated as abandoning the change-of-status request.
4. **Departure before a decision, and the $100,000 payment**
	USCIS's H-1B page says the $100,000 proclamation payment does not apply to a granted in-country change of status, but if USCIS finds the person ineligible for it, for instance because they departed before the decision, the payment applies. Together with the travel rule, this makes staying put during the pending petition the safe course ([[H-1B 100K Payment]]).
5. **A shortened OPT end date**
	If the record shows OPT ending September 30 though the H-1B start is later, the DSO can request a data fix from SEVIS.
6. **A layoff before the H-1B starts**
	The employer must withdraw the petition so that USCIS receives the withdrawal before the change takes effect. Then the student can use any remaining valid OPT. If USCIS does not receive it in time, the student must stop working, file Form I-539, and wait for approval.
## Interactions
- *STEM OPT:* a student with a cap-gap extension may apply for the STEM extension during it, but not once the cap-gap has ended through a denied petition and the 60-day period has begun ([[STEM OPT Extension]]).
- *Unemployment limits:* initial OPT allows 90 days, with 60 more under STEM OPT for 150 in all.
- *The fixed-period rule:* DHS's transition guidance says cap-gap students need no extension of stay and remain in F-1 until approval or April 1 ([[Duration of Status Rule]]).
- *The page is dated:* USCIS's cap-gap page was last updated January 17, 2025 and still uses duration of status wording, so re-check it if the fixed-period rule returns.
