## Where Things Stand
==ICE did not change the CPT regulation. It changed how DSOs must read it through two broadcast messages, and a court has not yet touched that.==
- *Blocked by a court:* the duration of status rule (since Sept 14) and the $100,000 H-1B payment (vacated June 8, in effect since the First Circuit denied a stay on July 24, appeals pending).
- *In force and untested:* the CPT guidance. The first court date is Nov 6.
- *Proposed, not law:* the $70,000 and $30,000 OPT fees, the $103,265 H-1B petition fee, the DOL wage rule.
- *Finalized in the last 30 days:* the end of automatic EAD extensions for renewals (Oct 14; the interim version has applied since Oct 30, 2025).

## CPT Guidance
1. **BCM 2608-01, Aug 12, 2026** ([ICE PDF](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/sevis/pdf/bcm260801.pdf))
	*Weight:* primary. Read from the PDF itself.
	==DSOs should authorize CPT "only if its absence would make the attainment of a degree an impossibility and if the work associated with CPT is required for all students pursuing that degree."== It also says SEVP "recognizes that the school, not the U.S. government, defines what is and is not required to complete a degree," which is the one opening it leaves schools.
2. **BCM 2608-02, Aug 24, 2026** ([ICE PDF](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/sevis/pdf/bcm_260802.pdf))
	*Weight:* primary.
	==Question 3 closes the elective route: an optional elective course makes the CPT optional, so it fails.== Question 6 requires a cooperative agreement between the employer and the school. Question 7 announces a confidential SEVP tipline for DSOs who feel pressured by their own leadership.
3. **ICE SEVIS "What's New" index** ([ice.gov](https://www.ice.gov/sevis/whats-new))
	*Weight:* primary, the official list of broadcast messages.
	==Bookmark this to check for a new CPT message before trusting any blog.== It lists the Oct 8 OPT fee message and the July 17 duration of status notice.
4. **8 CFR 214.2(f)(10), version dated July 1, 2026** ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/on/2026-07-01/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2))
	*Weight:* primary law.
	==The regulation's CPT sentence never says "required for all students": it says "an integral part of an established curriculum" and lists "required internship or practicum."== Use the July 1 version, not "current." The current eCFR view already prints the postponed duration of status wording (30 days) where the operative rule says 60.
## Lawsuit Over CPT
1. **Docket, AAU v. DHS, No. 1:26-cv-14520** ([CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/74919449/association-of-american-universities-v-us-department-of-homeland/))
	*Weight:* primary, RECAP copy of PACER, 24 entries through Oct 23.
	==Judge Julia E. Kobick set the hearing for Nov 6 at 3:00 PM and gave the government until Oct 28 to respond.== No ruling, no temporary restraining order, nothing that pauses the guidance.
2. **Complaint** ([PDF hosted by NAFSA](https://www.nafsa.org/sites/default/files/media/document/CPT%20Complaint%20AAU%20v.%20DHS%20.pdf))
	*Weight:* party filing, read in full.
	==Paragraph 5 states the old rule: CPT was allowed when it earned academic credit, or, when it earned none, when it was mandatory.== The Broadcasts move the "mandatory" test onto credit-bearing training too. Claims are Administrative Procedure Act counts: arbitrary and capricious, retroactive, contrary to law, no notice-and-comment. Paragraph 2 lists computer science among the affected fields.
3. **Memorandum supporting the injunction** ([AAU PDF](https://www.aau.edu/uploads/files/general/Memo-in-Support-of-PI-and-Stay-AAU-v-DHS.pdf))
	*Weight:* party filing, 56 pages, skimmed for the case number and dates, not read line by line.
	==Document 9-1, filed Oct 5, is where the harm evidence sits (mid-semester halts and cancelled placements).== Read it if the hearing outcome matters for a specific plan.
4. **NAFSA litigation page** ([nafsa.org](https://www.nafsa.org/legal-defense-CPT))
	*Weight:* plaintiff, last updated Oct 8.
	==Lists the four plaintiffs and counsel (Jenner & Block) and links the filings.== It gives the hearing date but not the time or judge, so use the docket for those.
## OPT Fee Proposal
1. **Federal Register, "Optional Practical Training Fees," 91 FR 64566** ([notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/08/2026-20660/optional-practical-training-fees))
	*Weight:* primary, read in full. Docket ICEB-2026-0100, RIN 1653-AB01.
	==The notice itself prices your choice: "a total fee of $100,000 would be assessed should the F-1 nonimmigrant engage in pre-completion and post-completion OPT, compared to $70,000 if the entire OPT period occurs post-completion," and DHS "anticipates that institutions would encourage F-1 nonimmigrants to use only post-completion OPT."== Every later recommendation costs $30,000, including a STEM extension, so by that rule pre, post, and STEM would total $130,000 (my arithmetic, not the notice's). DHS "acknowledges that the schools may pass the financial obligation" to F-1 students or employers. The fee would apply to recommendations dated on or after 60 days after a final rule.
2. **ICE BCM 2610-02, Oct 8** ([ICE PDF](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/sevis/pdf/bcm2610-02.pdf))
	*Weight:* primary.
	==It describes the $30,000 as a charge for "the second year of practical training," which is looser than the rule text, so the Federal Register wording governs.== It confirms Nov 9 and Dec 7.
3. **DHS press release, Oct 7** ([dhs.gov](https://www.dhs.gov/news/2026/10/07/dhs-proposes-fees-foreign-student-work-program-protect-american-jobs))
	*Weight:* primary, last updated Oct 20.
	==The stated purpose is to make schools "exercise greater oversight and selectivity," meaning the fee is a lever on schools, not students.==
4. **WR Immigration, "Fifteen Things to Know"** ([wolfsdorf.com](https://wolfsdorf.com/fifteen-things-to-know-about-dhss-proposed-opt-fees/))
	*Weight:* secondary, an immigration law firm.
	==The scale DHS used is 194,554 OPT and 95,384 STEM OPT participants in calendar 2024, and the notice concedes opportunity costs "if educational institutions are unable to pay or choose not to pay."== Cross-checked: all three figures and the pass-through language are in the notice. The firm's "$8.4 to $16.5 billion" range was not checked.
## Duration Of Status
1. **Federal Register final rule, 91 FR 44976, July 17, 2026** ([notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/17/2026-14439/establishing-a-fixed-time-period-of-admission-and-an-extension-of-stay-procedure-for-nonimmigrant))
	*Weight:* primary. Docket ICEB-2025-0001, RIN 1653-AA95.
	==Admission would be fixed to the I-20 end date, capped at four years, with an extension of stay needed for OPT.== Effective date Sept 15, 2026, now postponed. ## Graduating In 2028: The OPT Path
> [!NOTE]
> This assumes a May 2028 program end date on the I-20. Confirm the exact date with the DSO, because every window below counts from it. Dates are approximate offsets under the rules in force today, which are the pre-duration-of-status rules.

| When | Step | Rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| About Feb 2028 (90 days before end date) | Earliest USCIS filing for post-completion OPT, after the DSO recommendation is in SEVIS | File within 30 days of that recommendation | USCIS OPT page, I-765 page |
| About March 2028 (forecast) | FY2029 H-1B registration, employer picks the wage level | FY2027 ran March 4 to 19 | USCIS registration page |
| April 1, 2028 | Earliest cap-subject petition filing | Filing window at least 90 days; degree evidence can be a final transcript or registrar letter | USCIS cap season, FY2027 alert |
| May 2028 | Program end date | Starts every OPT clock | I-20 |
| About July 2028 (60 days after) | Last day to file OPT and latest requested start date | Under the postponed rule this shrinks to 30 days | USCIS I-765 page, 8 CFR dated July 1 |
| Oct 1, 2028 | Earliest H-1B start | Cap-gap carries OPT and F-1 status until the change of status takes effect, no later than April 1, 2029 | USCIS cap-gap page |
| About July 2029 (14 months after) | Outer limit for the initial 12 months of OPT | STEM extension is exempt | 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(A)(3) |
| Any time on OPT | Unemployment limit | 90 days on initial OPT, 150 with STEM | USCIS cap-gap page |
| Up to 90 days before OPT ends | STEM OPT filing | E-Verify employer and Form I-983 required | 8 CFR dated July 1 |
| About March 2029 and March 2030 (inference) | Later registration chances while on OPT and STEM OPT | Requires an employer willing to register each year | My reading of the above, not a source's statement |
### Sources Behind The Table
1. **USCIS, OPT for F-1 Students** ([uscis.gov](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/optional-practical-training-opt-for-f-1-students))
	*Weight:* primary, last updated Nov 25, 2024, so it predates the duration of status rule.
	==Pre-completion OPT is deducted from post-completion at a rate that depends on hours: one year part-time costs 6 months, one year full-time costs the whole 12.== File with USCIS up to 90 days before and no later than 60 days after the program end date, and within 30 days of the DSO recommendation. A timely STEM OPT filing gets an automatic 180-day extension.
2. **USCIS, Form I-765 page** ([uscis.gov](https://www.uscis.gov/i-765))
	*Weight:* primary.
	==The (c)(3)(B) row repeats the 90-days-before, 60-days-after window, confirming USCIS still applies the old rule while the new one is postponed.== EAD cards ship within about 2 weeks of approval, allow 30 days before inquiring.
3. **USCIS, Premium Processing** ([uscis.gov](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms/how-do-i-request-premium-processing))
	*Weight:* primary.
	==Premium processing is open to pre-completion, post-completion, and STEM OPT I-765s, with a 30-business-day guarantee.== The fee is on Form G-1055, which I did not read.
4. **Reddy Neumann Brown, "OPT Delays in 2026"** ([rnlawgroup.com](https://www.rnlawgroup.com/opt-delays-in-2026-why-the-14-month-rule-makes-a-timely-decision-essential/))
	*Weight:* secondary, an immigration law firm. The rule it cites is real: 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(A)(3) requires all OPT within 14 months of completion.
	==Because employment starts on the approval date, a late EAD permanently shortens the 12 months.== Its claim that USCIS delays now stretch to months is unverified, since USCIS's processing-times tool blocked my fetch.
5. **8 CFR 214.2(f)(10) to (12), July 1, 2026 version** ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/on/2026-07-01/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2))
	*Weight:* primary law.
	==STEM OPT requires an E-Verify employer, a signed Form I-983 training plan, and a new I-983 within 10 days of changing employers.== OPT grace after authorization ends is 60 days.
6. **USCIS, cap-gap page** ([uscis.gov](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations/extension-of-post-completion-optional-practical-training-opt-and-f-1-status-for-eligible-students))
	*Weight:* primary, last updated Jan 17, 2025.
	==Cap-gap extends OPT and F-1 status to April 1 of the H-1B fiscal year, covers only cap-subject petitions, and does not apply to cap-exempt employers such as universities.== Unemployment limits sit here too: 90 days on initial OPT, 150 with STEM OPT.
7. **Federal Register final rule, "Removal of Automatic Extensions for Certain EADs," 91 FR 65306** ([notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/14/2026-20955/removal-of-automatic-extensions-for-certain-employment-authorization-documents))
	*Weight:* primary, effective Oct 14, 2026.
	==In the categories it covers, an EAD no longer stays valid while a renewal is pending.== The text does not mention OPT or STEM OPT, and STEM OPT's own 180-day extension is a separate provision (8 CFR 274a.12(b)(6)(iv)), so I read it as unaffected. That is my reading, not a USCIS statement.
## H-1B Path For A 2028 Graduate
1. **USCIS, H-1B Cap Season** ([uscis.gov](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations/h-1b-cap-season))
	*Weight:* primary.
	==Wage level IV registrations enter the lottery 4 times, level III 3, level II 2, level I once.== If the degree is not yet awarded, the petition can use a final transcript or a registrar's letter confirming all requirements are met. Filing opens April 1 for a start of Oct 1.
2. **USCIS, H-1B Electronic Registration** ([uscis.gov](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations/h-1b-electronic-registration-process))
	*Weight:* primary.
	==FY2027 registration ran March 4 to 19, 2026, cost $215 per registration, and eligible registrations fell from 470,342 (FY2025) to 343,981 (FY2026).== Expect FY2029 to follow the same March pattern, which is a forecast.
3. **USCIS, FY2027 selection completed, Mar 31, 2026** ([uscis.gov](https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/alerts/fy-2027-h-1b-initial-registration-selection-process-completed))
	*Weight:* primary.
	==The petition filing window is "at least 90 days," so a May graduate can finish the degree before it closes.== That is my inference, not USCIS's statement.
4. **TRAC, "Fundamental Changes to H-1B Visas"** ([tracreports.org](https://tracreports.org/reports/768/))
	*Weight:* research organization at Syracuse University, built from the DHS rule's own data.
	==Modeled selection odds: about 15.3 percent at wage level I against 61.2 percent at level IV, versus 29.6 percent for everyone under the old random draw.== These are modeled, not actual FY2027 results.
5. **Proclamation 11069, Sept 18, 2026** ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/23/2026-19554/restriction-on-entry-of-certain-nonimmigrant-workers))
	*Weight:* primary.
	==It reports that registrations for beneficiaries with a U.S. master's rose from 45.1 to 66.1 percent of the total in one year, and that job offers at the lowest wage level were about 17.8 percent of selections.== Those are the government's figures and its own justification for the policy.
6. **USCIS, H-1B Specialty Occupations** ([uscis.gov](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/h-1b-specialty-occupations))
	*Weight:* primary.
	==The $100,000 payment "does not apply" to an in-U.S. change of status that is granted, which covers F-1 to H-1B.== It does apply if the change of status is denied or the person leaves the U.S. before it is decided.
7. **Court dockets for the $100,000 payment**
	*Weight:* primary. [First Circuit No. 26-1699](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73476893/state-of-california-v-mullin/), [Ninth Circuit No. 26-6667](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/74966923/global-nurse-force-et-al-v-trump-et-al/), [D.C. Circuit No. 25-5473](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72095497/chamber-of-commerce-of-the-united-states-of-ameri-v-dhs/).
	==Three appeals are pending in three circuits, and the D.C. Circuit has had argument since March 2026 with no decision, as of the parties' Oct 15 letters.== The First Circuit denied the government's stay on July 24 and denied reconsideration on Sept 21. I did not read the lower-court rulings, so I am not claiming the circuits disagree. The Northern District of California docket shows a September 30 order "granting in part and denying in part" a preliminary injunction in Global Nurse Force, which some summaries described as a vacatur.
8. **Federal Register proposals** ([$103,265 fee](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/25/2026-17324/fee-for-certain-h-1b-petitions), [DOL wage rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/27/2026-06017/improving-wage-protections-for-the-temporary-and-permanent-employment-of-certain-foreign-nationals), [no deference to prior approvals](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/14/2026-20941/removing-deference-to-prior-uscis-determinations-of-eligibility), [60-day grace period](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/11/2026-18631/eliminating-the-discretionary-60-day-grace-period))
	*Weight:* primary, all proposed.
	==The $103,265 fee would apply to all cap-subject petitions including the master's exemption, the new rule would end deference for H-1B extensions, and the grace-period rule lists E, H-1B, L-1, O-1 and TN but not F-1.== None is final. The first two are the ones that change a 2028 graduate's employer cost.
