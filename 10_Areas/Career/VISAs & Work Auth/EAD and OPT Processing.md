# EAD and OPT Processing
An OPT approval is only useful when the work permit card arrives, and the card's timing is the least controlled part of the whole track. This note covers the application form, the card, how long it takes, premium processing, the 14-month rule that turns delay into lost work months, and the October rule that ended automatic extensions. Read it before choosing a filing date.
## In Plain Words
OPT is only real when a physical card, the **EAD**, arrives in the mail. To get it, I ask my school for a new I-20, then I send the I-20 and a form (**I-765**) to USCIS with a fee, then I wait. The wait is the hardest part of the whole track. ISSS says USCIS now takes about 5 months, and the advisor said that is "now the normal amount of time." For a faster answer I can pay a second fee for **premium processing**, which forces USCIS to act within 30 business days (the advisor said "lately we've seen them pretty much take the entire 30 business days"). I cannot start work until I hold the card and its start date has come ([[OPT Playbook]], [[US Immigration Rules Explained]]).
## What It Costs And Who Does What
USCIS's fee schedule (edition 10/16/26) lists the general work permit application at $470 online or $520 on paper, and premium processing for OPT and STEM OPT at $1,780. ISSS's page lists no fee for the post-completion I-20, and tells students not to pay the $85 biometrics fee. UMN's online lookup for the current wait is USCIS's processing-times page, with the form I-765, the category (c)(3) for F-1 students, and the Potomac Service Center selected. ISSS makes the I-20 in up to 10 business days, and USCIS must receive my application within 30 days of that I-20's issue date or it denies it.
## The Form And The Card
OPT work authorization is requested on Form I-765, Application for Employment Authorization, and issued as an Employment Authorization Document, Form I-766. USCIS lists the F-1 categories as (c)(3)(A) for pre-completion OPT, (c)(3)(B) for post-completion OPT, and (c)(3)(C) for the 24-month STEM extension. The (c)(3)(B) row repeats the window: file up to 90 days before, but no later than 60 days after, the program end date, and within 30 days of the date the DSO enters the recommendation.
- *Before approval:* USCIS says a student "may not begin your pre- or post-completion OPT until after we approve your Form I-765 and you receive your Employment Authorization Document."
- *After approval:* the card should be produced within two weeks, and USCIS asks for a total of 30 days from approval before an inquiry. Case Status Online shows the delivery tracking number.
- *Start date:* employment authorization begins on the date requested or the date the application is adjudicated, whichever is later, and a student cannot request a start more than 60 days after the program end date.
## Processing Time
==The only fact the track can rely on is that the card arrives after a wait measured in months, not weeks, so the filing date has to be set backwards from the date work must start.== The CPT complaint says that as of September 28, 2026, USCIS's Case Processing Times page showed only 80 percent of F-1 student OPT applications completed within five months. That is a party's citation of a government page. USCIS's tool blocked my own automated fetch, so check the live figure by hand before relying on it. The ISSS advisor independently gave about 5 months on October 23, and ISSS's own pages say 3 to 5 months for OPT and about 3.5 to 4 months for the STEM extension. Earlier in the year, the advisor said, the wait was between 2 and 4 months.
A law firm writing in 2026 says delays have stretched from weeks into months and in some cases past a year. I could not verify the "past a year" claim, and I give it no weight beyond showing the direction of travel. The five-month figure is now backed by three sources: the court filing, the ISSS advisor, and ISSS's pages.
## The 14-Month Rule
The regulation requires all initial OPT to be completed within 14 months of completing study, except the 24-month STEM extension. Because employment starts at the later of the requested and approval dates, a late approval does not move the end date, so the 12 months shrink. A law firm's illustration is that a student who files promptly but waits five months can end with only about nine months of OPT, having been unable to work during the wait because a pending initial application does not authorize employment. The firm's conclusion that the card should arrive within about two months of graduation to preserve the full year follows from the regulation's arithmetic ([[OPT Program]]).
## Premium Processing
USCIS has accepted premium processing for pre-completion, post-completion, and STEM OPT applications since April 3, 2023, after a phase-in that began March 6. It is requested on Form I-907, online or on paper, either with the I-765 or later as an upgrade of a pending case.
- *The guarantee:* 30 business days for Form I-765, starting when USCIS receives a properly completed I-907 with the correct fee. The fee is set on Form G-1055, which I did not read.
- *What counts as action:* an approval, a denial, a notice of intent to deny, a request for evidence, or opening a fraud investigation. It guarantees action, not approval.
- *If evidence is requested:* the 30-day clock stops and restarts when the response arrives.
- *If missed:* USCIS refunds the premium fee.
## October 14: Automatic EAD Extensions Ended
The final rule "Removal of Automatic Extensions for Certain Employment Authorization Documents," effective October 14, 2026, finalizes an interim rule published October 30, 2025 without change. Before the interim rule, a timely renewal application could extend an expiring work permit for up to 540 days. A renewal filed on or after October 30, 2025 no longer does, unless the Code of Federal Regulations, another law, or a notice for Temporary Protected Status says so. It does not affect permits extended before that date.
I did not find OPT, STEM OPT, or F-1 named in the rule. The STEM 180-day extension lives in a different provision, 8 CFR 274a.12(b)(6)(iv), which USCIS's OPT page and the October 8 OPT fee notice still describe, so I read the STEM extension as unaffected. That is a reading, not a USCIS statement ([[STEM OPT Extension]]).
## The Fixed-Period Rule's Effect
If the postponed duration of status rule takes effect, a student needing OPT beyond the I-20 end date must apply for an extension of stay on Form I-539 and apply for employment authorization at the same time. A student already in the country under duration of status on the effective date who files for post-completion OPT or STEM OPT on or before March 18, 2027 is exempt from the extension-of-stay application, a date fixed in the rule's text that DHS may extend. A student who travels and is readmitted under the fixed period before filing is not exempt ([[Duration of Status Rule]]).
## Words Used In This Note
- *EAD:* Employment Authorization Document, the work permit card.
- *Request for evidence:* a USCIS letter asking for more documents; it stops the clock.
- *Adjudicate:* to decide an application.
- *Interim final rule:* a rule that takes effect before the public comments are read.
## Failure Modes
- Filing at the last day of the window because "60 days after" feels safe, then losing months to the 14-month cap.
- Planning a start date around the card's expected arrival.
- Paying for premium processing and treating the 30 business days as an approval date.
- Citing a blog's processing estimate instead of the live page.
