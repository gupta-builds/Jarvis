# F-1 Employment Options
An F-1 student may not work in the United States except through a short list of exceptions, and each one has its own authorizer, hours, and failure mode. This note is the full list, so a decision like [[Junior Year Internship Decision]] can compare options on one page. Read it before accepting any offer, paid or not, that starts before graduation.
## In Plain Words
The rule for F-1 students is simple to state: no work unless a specific exception applies. Each exception is a different permission, from a different office, with different hours. Working without the right one, even for no pay, can put my F-1 status and my later OPT at risk. The table below is the complete list, and the sections after it explain each in plain terms. "Authorizer" means who gives the permission: it is the law itself for on-campus work, my school for CPT, and the government agency USCIS for OPT.
## The Options Compared
| Option | Who authorizes | Where and when | Hours | Status on 2026-10-23 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| On-campus employment | Incident to status, no application | School premises or an educationally affiliated site | 20 a week in session, full-time in breaks | Open |
| Curricular practical training | The school's DSO | Before graduation, one employer and period per authorization | Part-time or full-time as authorized | UMN paused all applications; only required experiences considered |
| Pre-completion OPT | USCIS after a DSO recommendation | Before graduation, any related job | 20 in session, full-time in breaks | Open, with costs |
| Post-completion OPT | USCIS after a DSO recommendation | After the program ends | Part-time or full-time | Open |
| STEM OPT extension | USCIS after a DSO recommendation | After 12 months of post-completion OPT | At least 20 a week | Open |
| Severe economic hardship | USCIS after a DSO recommendation | Off campus, after one academic year | 20 in session, full-time in breaks | Open, rare |
| Internship with an international organization | USCIS after a DSO certification | Recognized international organizations only | As authorized | Open, rare |
## On-Campus Employment
==On-campus work needs no application, but it must sit on the school's premises or at an educationally affiliated site, and it caps at 20 hours a week while classes are in session.== The regulation allows on-location commercial firms that serve students, such as the bookstore or cafeteria. It excludes a firm like a construction company building a school building, which provides no direct student services.
Work at an off-campus location counts only when the educational affiliation is associated with the school's established curriculum or related to contractually funded research projects at the post-graduate level. The post-graduate wording is easy to miss. It means an undergraduate cannot assume a company that funds a university lab project qualifies. The employment must also not displace United States residents, and the student may work full-time when school is not in session or during the annual vacation.
ISSS adds that students cannot hold work-study positions and that on-campus eligibility ends on the day all degree requirements are complete. A student who applied for post-completion OPT but does not finish by the date on that application loses on-campus eligibility, unless the job is related to their field and the OPT is authorized. This is the only option that works the summer after junior year without a USCIS wait, and it is the one least affected by the 2026 changes ([[Junior Year Internship Decision]]).
## Curricular Practical Training
CPT is authorized by the DSO, not USCIS, and it is employer-specific and date-specific. The 2026 dispute is about what makes a training "integral to an established curriculum." Full detail lives in [[CPT Program]], the [[CPT Broadcast Messages]], and the [[CPT Lawsuit]]. The short version is that SEVP now says only training required of every student in the degree qualifies, and UMN has paused every application while it reviews that guidance.
## Optional Practical Training
OPT is the USCIS-issued work permit for training directly related to the major. A DSO recommends it, the student files Form I-765, and employment cannot start before the approved start date on the card ([[EAD and OPT Processing]]). It comes in three forms. [[Pre-Completion OPT]] is used during the degree, [[OPT Program|post-completion OPT]] after it, and the [[STEM OPT Extension]] adds 24 months for eligible STEM degrees. All OPT is capped at 12 months per education level, and time used before graduation is deducted. Two ISSS facts from the meeting matter here. During the school term, pre-completion OPT is only part-time (20 hours a week or less), and that 20 hours includes any on-campus job. And a part-time stretch in term and a full-time stretch in the summer are two separate applications.
## Hardship And International Organizations
Off-campus work for severe economic hardship requires an unforeseen financial change outside the student's control, a full academic year in status, good standing, and proof that on-campus work is unavailable or insufficient. The DSO recommends it in one-year intervals and USCIS decides. ISSS says the fee applies and USCIS will likely take 4 to 7 months, so it is not a route to a planned internship.
An offer from a recognized international organization under the International Organizations Immunities Act has its own USCIS application path. That is a narrow case and not relevant to a typical software internship.
## What Is Not Authorized
- Any off-campus job that is not CPT, OPT, hardship, or an international organization, whether paid, stipended, or compensated in kind. ISSS lists paid internships, summer and break work, stipends or bonuses, and free housing for caretaking.
- Working before the authorization is approved and the start date on it has arrived. The I-20 endorsement (CPT) or the EAD start date (OPT) is the gate, not the offer letter.
- Working more than 20 hours a week during the term on campus or on pre-completion OPT.
- Remote work done while sitting in the U.S. That is U.S. employment, so it needs CPT or OPT. The ISSS advisor drew the line at location: "you are not tied by F-1 regulations when you physically are in another country," so an internship done while physically abroad is not covered by these rules. I still need ISSS to confirm how it affects my status, summer enrollment, and re-entry ([[ISSS October Meeting Brief]], [[Junior Year Internship Decision]]).
- Volunteer or unpaid roles described as jobs. The advisor warned that students have been asked by the government to explain work done without authorization, and that a group project should not be presented as an employment opportunity.
## Words Used In This Note
- *Incident to status:* allowed automatically because of F-1 status, with no application.
- *Authorization:* the specific permission that makes a job legal.
- *Educationally affiliated site:* an off-campus place tied to the curriculum or to contracted research.
- *Unauthorized employment:* working without the right permission, paid or not.
## UMN Snapshot
ISSS says international students "must always obtain work authorization to work off-campus," that off-campus work must relate to the UMN major, and that it encourages advising early in the job search. The ISSS pages were last updated between September 2026 and, for on-campus work, February 2025, and the CPT text says it "may be outdated and will be updated soon." The UMN rewrite is queued, so this snapshot is a reference point and not a current ruling.
