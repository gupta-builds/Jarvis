# F-1 Visa
F-1 is the student status the whole track hangs from: CPT, OPT, STEM OPT, cap-gap, and finally H-1B are either exceptions carved out of it or exits from it. This note holds the rules of the status itself so the work-authorization notes can assume them. Read it first when another note says "maintain status" and you need to know what that costs.
## What The Status Is
==F-1 status is granted for one purpose, a full course of study at a school certified by the Student and Exchange Visitor Program, so every right to work is an exception carved out of that purpose.== The statute describes a bona fide student with a residence abroad who enters "temporarily and solely for the purpose of pursuing" that course of study, as DHS restates it in the [[OPT Fee Proposal|OPT fee notice]]. That is the opposite design from H-1B, where the status exists because of a job and ends when the job does ([[H1B Visa]]).
DHS leans on this purpose in its current rulemaking. The OPT fee notice says the F-1 visa "was never intended to provide a pathway to employment in the United States." That sentence is the government's position, not settled law, but it explains why each 2026 change tightens the employment exceptions rather than the study itself ([[Current News]]).
## The Documents That Define Status
1. **Form I-20**
	The "Certificate of Eligibility for Nonimmigrant Student Status," issued by a [[SEVP and DSOs|designated school official]]. The DSO certifies under penalty of perjury that it is true and correct, which is why DSOs treat every endorsement as personal liability ([[CPT Broadcast Messages]]). Its employment page carries the CPT and OPT endorsements, and a student may begin CPT only after receiving an I-20 with the DSO's endorsement.
2. **SEVIS record**
	The government database the DSO updates. For OPT, the recommendation must be entered in SEVIS before the student can apply to USCIS, and the student must then receive a new I-20 showing it ([[OPT Program]]).
3. **Form I-94**
	The arrival and departure record. Today it shows "D/S" (duration of status). Under the postponed fixed-period rule it would show an Admit Until Date instead ([[Duration of Status Rule]]).
4. **Employment Authorization Document (Form I-766)**
	The work permit USCIS issues for OPT, STEM OPT, and the rare hardship category. CPT does not use one: the August broadcast says students "are not expected to obtain EADs for this training" ([[EAD and OPT Processing]]).
## Maintaining Status
==Status survives on three habits: a full course of study every term, normal progress toward the degree, and no work that a DSO or USCIS has not authorized.==
- *Full course of study:* for an undergraduate at a college or university, at least 12 semester or quarter hours per term. Online or distance classes count for no more than one class or three credits per term.
- *Reduced load:* only with the DSO's approval before the student drops, and only for listed reasons. Initial academic difficulty (once per program level), a documented medical condition (up to 12 months in total), or the final term when fewer courses are needed. Dropping below full-time without approval puts the student out of status.
- *Vacations:* the annual or summer vacation counts as in status if the student is eligible and intends to register for the next term. This is why summer work is a legal question and not a status question ([[F-1 Employment Options]]).
- *Normal progress:* a program that will not finish by the I-20 end date needs a program extension requested before that date, for a compelling academic reason, documented illness, or circumstances beyond the student's control.
- *Reinstatement:* USCIS may consider reinstating a student on Form I-539 with a DSO recommendation. It is a discretionary last resort, not a right.
- *On-campus assistantships:* work under a scholarship, fellowship, or assistantship counts as part of the academic program for the full-course-of-study test.
## Time Limits And Grace Periods
The rules in force today are the duration of status rules. A student may enter up to 30 days before the program start date and stay while pursuing a full course of study or authorized practical training. After studies and any practical training end there is a 60-day period to prepare for departure or transfer, and 15 days if the DSO authorized a withdrawal. A student who fails to maintain status gets no departure period.
The fixed-period rule would replace this with an Admit Until Date of up to four years plus 30 days to arrive and 30 days to depart. A federal court postponed it on September 14, so the 60-day period is the operative one for now ([[Duration of Status Rule]]). Dates in this note and in [[2028 Graduation Timeline]] use the 60-day rules and flag where the 30-day version would change them.
> [!WARNING]
> The "current" eCFR view of 8 CFR 214.2 already prints the postponed rule's 30-day OPT filing and start-date wording. Use the version dated 2026-07-01 for the rules actually in force.

## Where I Stand
I am at the University of Minnesota Twin Cities, a junior in Fall 2026, with a computer science degree. The internship research notes already filter for a Spring 2028 graduation ([[Companies giving OPT & CPT]]), so every window here counts from a program end date in spring 2028. The exact date on the I-20 decides all of them, so confirm it with ISSS before trusting a calendar.
Practical training requires one full academic year of full-time enrollment, which a junior has met on its face. Computer Science (CIP 11.0701) is on the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List, but the code printed on the I-20 is what counts ([[STEM OPT Extension]]). The University of Minnesota is also a member of the Association of American Universities, one of the plaintiffs challenging the CPT guidance ([[CPT Lawsuit]]).
## Failure Modes
- Working off campus without authorization, including unpaid or benefit-in-kind work. ISSS lists a free-rent caretaking job as employment that needs authorization.
- Dropping below full-time without the DSO's approval first.
- Leaving the country during the 60-day period before filing for OPT. ISSS warns this ends the rest of the period and the eligibility to apply.
- Trusting a university page without its date. Several pages still say 60 days, others already say 30, and the court order sits between them.
- Treating a lawsuit filing as a rule change. A complaint changes nothing until a court rules ([[Visa Litigation Tracker]]).
