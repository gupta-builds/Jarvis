# H-1B 100K Payment
A presidential proclamation told employers to pay $100,000 for certain H-1B workers, courts blocked the government from collecting it, and the president extended it anyway on September 18. This note separates what the proclamation says, who USCIS says it covers, what the courts have actually done, and what is still open. It matters to me mainly for one narrow reason: the payment does not reach an F-1 student changing status inside the country. Read it before any employer says "the $100,000 fee" is why they do not sponsor.
## The Text
==The proclamation restricts the entry of H-1B workers abroad unless their employer pays $100,000, so it was written as a limit on who may enter, not as a fee on every H-1B petition.== Proclamation 10973 of September 19, 2025 took effect September 21, 2025. Proclamation 11069 of September 18, 2026 extends it twelve months, to 12:01 AM Eastern on September 21, 2027.
- *Section 1:* entry as a specialty-occupation worker is restricted except for aliens whose petitions are accompanied or supplemented by a $100,000 payment. DHS is to restrict decisions on unpaid petitions for workers currently outside the United States.
- *Exceptions:* the Secretary of Homeland Security may exempt an individual, all workers at a company, or all in an industry, if hiring is in the national interest and poses no threat to security or welfare. USCIS says exceptions are "extraordinarily rare" and are requested at H1BExceptions@hq.dhs.gov.
- *Section 2:* employers must obtain and keep documentation of payment before filing for a worker outside the country, and State is to approve only visa applications with proof of payment.
- *Section 3:* it applies only to people who enter or attempt to enter after the effective date, including by consular notification, notification at a port of entry, pre-flight inspection, or pre-clearance. Within 30 days after the next H-1B lottery, the Secretaries of State, Labor, Homeland Security and the Attorney General must recommend whether to extend again.
The preamble explains the government's reasoning. Payment had been made for "over 700 petitions." Registrations by the largest IT staffing and outsourcing firms fell from 24,946 to 2,055, a 92 percent drop, and consular processing requests fell nearly 97 percent between the FY2025 and FY2027 cap seasons. Unemployment for recent college graduates was 5.7 percent in June 2026, and underemployment 42 percent. A companion executive order, 14431, adds interagency review of layoffs and wage data ([[H1B Visa]]).
## Who It Covers, According To USCIS
USCIS's own H-1B page is the clearest statement I found.
- It covers petitions filed on or after September 21, 2025 for beneficiaries outside the United States who do not hold a valid H-1B visa, and petitions that ask for consular notification, port of entry notification, or pre-flight inspection for someone in the country.
- It "does not apply" to a petition for an amendment, change of status, or extension of stay inside the United States where the alien is granted it. A later departure and visa application on that approved petition does not make the person subject to the payment.
- It does apply if a change-of-status petition is filed and USCIS finds the person ineligible, for example because they were not in valid status or **left the country before the decision**.
- It does not apply to existing valid H-1B visas or to petitions filed before September 21, 2025.
- Payment is made through pay.gov before filing, and a petition subject to it without proof is to be denied, subject to the court orders below.
For an F-1 student on OPT with a timely change-of-status petition, the payment should not apply, as long as the student stays and the petition is granted ([[Cap-Gap Extension]]).
## What The Courts Have Done
1. **State of California v. Mullin, D. Mass. No. 1:25-cv-13829**
	Judge Sorokin ruled on cross-motions for summary judgment and entered judgment on June 8, 2026. USCIS describes the result as vacating agency guidance that implemented the payment. He denied a stay but stayed his order administratively until the First Circuit could rule. The government appealed (First Circuit No. 26-1699, docketed June 12), and on July 24 the First Circuit denied the government's motion to stay, after which DHS said it "will comply with the court's order while DHS considers next steps" and "If this order is later lifted, DHS still plans to collect the payment." Plaintiffs include states such as Massachusetts, California and Minnesota. On September 21 the First Circuit denied the government's motion for reconsideration, and briefing is under way.
2. **Global Nurse Force v. Trump, N.D. Cal. No. 4:25-cv-08454**
	On September 30, 2026 Judge Gilliam "GRANT[ED] IN PART AND DENY[ED] IN PART" a motion for a preliminary injunction and denied class certification and a motion to dismiss. The government appealed to the Ninth Circuit (No. 26-6667, October 9), moved to stay proceedings, and has a hearing on that motion on January 14, 2027. I read the docket entries, not the opinion, so I do not state the scope of the injunction.
3. **Chamber of Commerce and AAU v. DHS, D.C. Cir. No. 25-5473**
	The D.C. Circuit held argument before Judges Wilkins, Katsas and Childs, entered March 9, 2026. The parties filed Rule 28(j) letters on September 25, about the new proclamation, and October 6, about the Ninth Circuit ruling, with government responses as late as October 15. No decision appears on the docket. I did not read the lower-court ruling that is on appeal.
The AAU is an appellant in the third case, which ties it to my school's membership, though that connection matters little to the $100,000 itself.
## The Other Fee
DHS has separately proposed a $103,265 fee on every cap-subject petition, including those eligible for the master's exemption and including change-of-status cases. It is stated to be in addition to any proclamation payment, so a petitioner could owe both. It is only proposed ([[H-1B Rule Proposals]]).
## What It Means For Me
- *If I stay on a change-of-status petition,* the $100,000 payment should not apply to me whether or not the courts lift their orders.
- *If I leave before the decision,* USCIS says the payment applies. That makes travel during the pending petition risky on two counts.
- *Expiry is September 21, 2027,* before my first cap season in March 2028, unless renewed. The extension decision is due to follow the March 2027 lottery, so a signal should appear in spring 2027.
- *Employers may still hesitate,* because the legal state is unsettled and the proposed $103,265 fee is separate.
