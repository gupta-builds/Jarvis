# OPT Program
Optional Practical Training is the F-1 work permit I will actually spend after graduation, and the bridge to H-1B. The vault uses this note as the hub for everything OPT: the standing rules, the clocks, the 2026 changes, and the traps that cost people months of work authorization. Read it before the semester I request a recommendation, and whenever a job posting says anything about sponsorship.
## What OPT Is
==OPT is a work permit the student already has the right to request, not sponsorship from an employer, and it is spent in 12-month units by the calendar whether or not a job is found.== It is temporary employment directly related to the major. USCIS says an eligible student can receive up to 12 months of OPT before completing studies, after completing them, or split across both, and all pre-completion time is deducted from post-completion time. A student becomes eligible for another 12 months on moving to a higher education level.
That is why the vault's job-screening rule reads "no visa sponsorship" as "no H-1B," not "no OPT." OPT is authorization the student holds, and the screening notes exclude a posting only on an explicit citizenship, clearance, or "OPT not accepted" statement ([[Companies giving OPT & CPT]]).
## The Three Forms
- [[Pre-Completion OPT]] happens during the degree, limited to 20 hours in term and full-time in vacations, and it consumes the same 12 months.
- Post-completion OPT starts after the program ends, at least 20 hours a week, and can be followed by the [[STEM OPT Extension]].
- The STEM extension adds 24 months for an eligible degree with an E-Verify employer and a training plan.
## The Steps For Post-Completion OPT
1. **Request the recommendation**
	The student asks the DSO, who enters the recommendation in SEVIS and issues a new I-20. UMN says to request it in MyISSS 100 days before the expected completion and to finish the ISSS OPT workshop first.
2. **File Form I-765 with USCIS**
	Within 30 days of the DSO's SEVIS entry, and between 90 days before and 60 days after the program end date. UMN adds: do not file until the new I-20 is in hand. USCIS warns that photos must be unmounted and unretouched.
3. **Optionally upgrade to premium processing**
	Form I-907 buys a 30-business-day guarantee for OPT applications ([[EAD and OPT Processing]]).
4. **Wait for the card**
	No work is allowed until the approved start date on the EAD. The permit begins on the requested date or the approval date, whichever is later, and the requested date cannot be more than 60 days after the program end date.
5. **Work and report**
	The job must be directly related to the major. The student reports name, address, and employment interruptions to the DSO.
6. **Plan the exit**
	STEM extension, H-1B with cap-gap, or the 60-day period to leave ([[Cap-Gap Extension]]).
## The Clocks
| Clock | Rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total OPT | 12 months per education level | 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10) |
| Outer limit | All initial OPT within 14 months of completing study | 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(A)(3) |
| File with USCIS | 90 days before to 60 days after the program end date | USCIS OPT and I-765 pages |
| After DSO recommendation | File within 30 days | USCIS OPT page |
| Start date | No more than 60 days after the program end date | 8 CFR 214.2(f)(11)(i)(D), version of July 1 |
| Unemployment | 90 days on initial OPT, 150 with STEM | USCIS cap-gap page |
| After OPT ends | 60 days to depart or change status | 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(iv) |
| STEM filing | Up to 90 days before OPT ends, within 60 days of the DSO recommendation | USCIS OPT page |
## What 2026 Changed Or Proposes
- A proposed fee of $70,000 for first OPT and $30,000 for each later OPT, charged to the school, in which DHS itself models a scenario where schools recommend only STEM students ([[OPT Fee Proposal]]).
- A final rule that ends automatic extensions for renewal work permits, which does not name OPT but changes the renewal landscape ([[EAD and OPT Processing]]).
- A fixed-period-of-admission rule that would shrink the post-completion windows to 30 days and add an extension-of-stay application, currently postponed by a court ([[Duration of Status Rule]]).
- Slow processing: the CPT complaint cites USCIS data from September 28 showing only 80 percent of F-1 student OPT applications done within five months.
The OPT fee notice also repeats critics' concern about payroll taxes. F-1 students on practical training are generally not subject to Social Security and Medicare taxes until after their fifth calendar year in F-1 status, which saves an employer 6.2 percent of pay up to the taxable wage base plus 1.45 percent of total pay. The notice treats that as an incentive to hire F-1 workers over U.S. workers.
## UMN Snapshot
ISSS says OPT lets a student work "either before or after degree completion in a job directly related to their major area of study" and that USCIS authorizes it. It warns that a student who departs the U.S. during the 60-day period before filing "effectively ends" their F-1 status and eligibility to apply. It lets OPT students keep their UMN email until work authorization ends plus 90 days, and asks to be told if a student decides not to use OPT. These pages were updated in September 2026 and are queued for review.
## Failure Modes
- Filing outside the 90-days-before to 60-days-after window, or more than 30 days after the DSO entry.
- Leaving the country in the 60-day period before OPT is approved.
- Letting a slow adjudication run into the 14-month limit, since the lost months do not come back.
- Using all 12 months before graduation and losing the STEM extension ([[Pre-Completion OPT]]).
- Counting unemployment days without checking the 90-day limit.
- Assuming a job offer is authorization.
