# Pre-Completion OPT
Pre-completion OPT is the only off-campus work route still open for a summer internship now that CPT is paused, and it is the one I have decided to avoid before graduation. This note records the full price of using it: lost OPT months, lost STEM eligibility, a larger proposed fee, and an approval clock that can outlast the internship. Read it when an offer arrives that can only start under OPT.
## How It Works
==Pre-completion OPT is ordinary OPT used before the degree is finished, and every month of it is deducted from the post-completion OPT that follows.== A student may apply once they have completed a full academic year of full-time enrollment. The regulation lets the student file Form I-765 up to 90 days before that anniversary as long as work does not start sooner, and the plaintiffs describe SEVP guidance as setting the earliest filing at 90 days before the requested start date. The student first needs a DSO recommendation and a new I-20. USCIS then adjudicates, and no work starts before the card's approved start date.
- *Hours:* 20 or fewer per week while school is in session. Full-time when school is not in session, such as the summer. UMN says summer may be part-time or full-time.
- *Course load:* the student must still carry a full course of study, because OPT does not replace enrollment.
- *Job:* directly related to the major, but the school does not pre-approve the employer, unlike CPT.
- *Unemployment:* pre-completion OPT has no unemployment limit; post-completion and STEM OPT do.
## What It Costs
1. **Months**
	USCIS is precise about the deduction. One year of part-time pre-completion OPT (20 hours a week) reduces the full-time OPT still available by six months, half the authorized year. One year of full-time pre-completion OPT reduces it by the whole year, and a student in that position "would not be entitled to any period of post-completion OPT." So a 12-week full-time summer costs about three months of post-completion OPT, and a part-time term costs half of that. Those figures are my arithmetic from USCIS's rule.
2. **STEM eligibility**
	ISSS says STEM extensions are not available for pre-completion OPT, that a student must be on post-completion OPT to apply for STEM OPT, and that using all 12 months before graduation loses the opportunity ([[STEM OPT Extension]]).
3. **Money, if the fee is finalized**
	The proposed rule charges $70,000 to the school on the first OPT recommendation of any kind and $30,000 on each later one. DHS spells out that pre-completion followed by post-completion OPT would total $100,000, against $70,000 if all OPT is post-completion, and says it "anticipates that institutions would encourage F-1 nonimmigrants to use only post-completion OPT." Add a STEM extension and the proposed rule's own per-recommendation arithmetic gives $130,000 (my sum, not the notice's). The fee has not been adopted and would apply to recommendations dated after its effective date ([[OPT Fee Proposal]]).
4. **Time**
	The plaintiffs in the CPT case say pre-completion OPT is a poor substitute because a student cannot apply until 90 days before the start and USCIS data showed only 80 percent of F-1 student OPT applications finished within five months as of September 28. Premium processing offers a 30-business-day guarantee ([[EAD and OPT Processing]]).
## The Summer 2027 Timeline
This is analysis built from the rules above. For a mid-June start, the earliest filing is about mid-March 2027, and the DSO recommendation must exist first (UMN's page says to request it in MyISSS 100 days before the start, about early March). Without premium processing, a five-month wait runs past the end of the summer. With it, 30 business days is about six weeks, which fits only if nothing delays the file or triggers a request for evidence. An offer that depends on this route has a real chance of arriving after its own start date.
## Where It Still Makes Sense
- A strong offer that cannot be done any other way, where the part-time deduction is small.
- A situation in which the fee proposal is withdrawn or scaled back, and the STEM extension is not at risk because enough months remain.
## Failure Modes
- Using all 12 months before graduation, which forfeits STEM OPT.
- Counting on the card in time and committing to the start date before it arrives.
- Assuming "20 hours a week" applies in the summer. It does not; vacation work may be full-time, which deducts faster.
- Forgetting that the approved OPT is deducted even if no internship materializes ([[Junior Year Internship Decision]]).
