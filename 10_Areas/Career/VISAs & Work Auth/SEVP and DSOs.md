# SEVP and DSOs
Most F-1 rules are written in a regulation, but they are applied by two kinds of people: federal staff in the Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP) and the school officials it certifies. This note explains who they are, what each can do to a student and to a school, and why a policy change reaches me through my own school's advisors before it reaches me through the law. Read it when a decision turns on whether ISSS "will approve" something.
## In Plain Words
Two groups of people decide what an international student can actually do. The first is **SEVP**, a government office that approves schools and watches the data. The second is the school's own staff, called **DSOs**, who sign each student's papers. If a student has a question about work, the DSO is the person to ask, and at UMN that is an advisor at ISSS. I met one on October 23. She was careful to say what she could and could not answer: she handles work authorization, she is "not an H-1B advisor," and she sent resume and employer questions to Career Services. ISSS offers drop-in hours, email, and appointments ([[ISSS October Meeting Brief]], [[US Immigration Rules Explained]]).
## The Two Layers
==SEVP certifies schools and the schools' designated officials certify students, so a federal policy reaches a student as a school's decision about what it dares to sign.== SEVP is the ICE program office that administers the F-1 program for DHS. It certifies each school to enroll F-1 students under 8 CFR 214.3, tracks students in the SEVIS database, and publishes guidance as broadcast messages and on its public site, Study in the States.
A **designated school official** (DSO) is a regular school employee whom the school nominates and the government approves. A school must name a principal DSO, and it may name more. The CPT complaint lists what a DSO does: issues the Form I-20, maintains each student's SEVIS record, and advises on status, employment, and practical training. At UMN that office is International Student and Scholar Services (ISSS), and students make requests through the MyISSS portal.
## What A DSO Signs
Every I-20 is certified under penalty of perjury as true and correct. Authorizing CPT means re-signing the I-20 with the training details, and recommending OPT means entering the recommendation in SEVIS and issuing a new I-20 before the student can file with USCIS. When a school petitions for certification or recertification, every DSO attests that they intend to comply with all SEVP regulations.
The August broadcast leans on exactly this: it says a DSO who signs a CPT I-20 is "certifying under penalty of perjury" that the training is required or otherwise integral to the curriculum. That is why the August guidance changed behavior within days even though it changed no regulation ([[CPT Broadcast Messages]]).
## What SEVP Can Do To A School
A school that loses SEVP certification immediately loses the ability to enroll new F-1 students, and its current F-1 students would have to transfer, because status depends on studying at a certified school (8 CFR 214.4(i), as the [[CPT Lawsuit|complaint]] describes it). Both August messages name withdrawal of certification as a consequence. Short of that, SEVP field representatives visit each school at least once a year to review records and provide training.
The OPT fee notice gives the scale: about 6,186 SEVP-certified schools as of June 2026, split into roughly 60 territories that each typically have one field representative. DHS says that is too few people to review the number of OPT employers, which is part of its argument for putting a price on OPT instead ([[OPT Fee Proposal]]).
## Broadcast Messages Are Not Regulations
A broadcast message is an email to DSOs, also posted on ICE's SEVIS pages, with a number such as 2608-01. Each carries a disclaimer that it "is not a substitute for applicable legal requirements, nor is it itself a rule or a final action by SEVP" and creates no enforceable right. In 2026 that disclaimer is the legal hinge. The plaintiffs argue the CPT messages are in substance a binding rule issued without notice and comment, while the messages say they only remind DSOs of existing law.
Three 2026 messages matter. BCM 2608-01 and 2608-02 on CPT, and BCM 2610-02, which announces the OPT fee proposal. ICE keeps the official list on its [SEVIS What's New page](https://www.ice.gov/sevis/whats-new), and that list is the first place to check for a new one.
## Reporting Channels In The Messages
BCM 2608-01 tells employers, DSOs, students, and the public to report suspected employment fraud, practical training fraud, or schools or students abusing the regulations to SEVPFraud@hsi.dhs.gov, an address at Homeland Security Investigations. BCM 2608-02 adds a confidential route for DSOs who feel pressured by school leadership to make attestations they believe are non-compliant, through the SEVP Response Center at 703-603-3400 or 1-800-892-4829, or SEVP@ice.dhs.gov, open Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Eastern. Both are in the primary text.
## Why This Layer Is The Binding Constraint
The plaintiffs report that at least one DSO with more than twenty years of experience considered resigning, and that others became reluctant to authorize even training they believed plainly qualified. Yale's office states that since August 24 it processes CPT only when the training is required of all students in the program. UMN paused every application. Those are separate institutional decisions made under the same threat.
The OPT fee proposal shifts the same pressure to a new point. Under it a DSO cannot enter an OPT recommendation in SEVIS until the school has paid, so the school's willingness to pay becomes the gate ([[OPT Fee Proposal]]).
> [!NOTE]
> A court order in the CPT case would bind the government, but each school would still decide how fast to restart authorizations, as the fear of retroactive scrutiny the plaintiffs describe does not vanish on the day of a ruling. Expect ISSS to move after a ruling, not with it.
## Words Used In This Note
- *SEVP:* the Student and Exchange Visitor Program, an office inside ICE.
- *SEVIS:* the database where my record lives.
- *DSO and PDSO:* the school official who signs my I-20, and the lead one.
- *Certification:* SEVP's approval of a school to enroll F-1 students.
- *Broadcast message:* an email from SEVP to DSOs that explains how to apply a rule.
## Working With ISSS
- Ask in writing, and keep the reply. Verbal assurances do not protect a student when the DSO signs under perjury.
- Request a recommendation through the portal before any application to USCIS. UMN's page says not to submit an OPT application until the new I-20 with the recommendation is in hand ([[OPT Program]]).
- Do not accept a start date before the I-20 endorsement or EAD start date exists ([[F-1 Employment Options]]).
