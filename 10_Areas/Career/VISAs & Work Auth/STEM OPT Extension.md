# STEM OPT Extension
The STEM extension turns 12 months of post-graduation work authorization into 36, which is what buys three tries at the H-1B lottery instead of one. It is also the part of OPT the government is watching most closely, because it has grown far beyond what DHS forecast. Read this before choosing a first employer after graduation, since that employer shapes eligibility for the next 24 months.
## What It Is
==The STEM extension is 24 more months of work authorization for a graduate whose degree is on the DHS STEM list, available only to someone already on post-completion OPT with an employer that uses E-Verify and signs a training plan.== The regulation caps a student at two lifetime STEM extensions, and a second one must rest on a higher-level degree. The extension begins the day after the initial OPT ends and runs 24 months, regardless of the date the approval arrives.
## Eligibility
- *The degree:* a bachelor's, master's, or doctorate from an accredited U.S. institution, in a field on the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List. Computer Science, CIP 11.0701, is on the July 22, 2024 list, as are Computer and Information Sciences, General (11.0101) and Information Technology (11.0103). The list is checked at the moment the DSO recommends the extension, using the degree code in SEVIS, so the code on my I-20 is what counts.
- *The base OPT:* an initial grant of post-completion OPT. Pre-completion OPT does not qualify, and a student cannot apply once a cap-gap extension has ended with a denied petition and the 60-day period has begun.
- *The employer:* enrolled in E-Verify and in good standing, with an employer identification number.
- *A prior degree:* if the extension rests on an earlier degree, it must be from a certified, accredited U.S. school and conferred within the previous 10 years.
## What The Employer Signs
Form I-983 is the training plan. The student and employer identify goals, specific skills to be taught, a performance evaluation process, and methods of oversight and supervision, and the employer attests to three things. It has enough resources and personnel to provide the training at the stated location. The student will not replace a full- or part-time U.S. worker, temporary or permanent. And the opportunity assists the student in reaching the training goals.
- *Pay and terms:* duties, hours, and compensation must be commensurate with those of the employer's similarly situated U.S. workers in the area.
- *Hours:* at least 20 hours per week.
- *Reporting:* the employer must report a termination or departure to the DSO within five business days.
- *New employer:* the student submits a new Form I-983 within 10 days of starting and needs a new DSO recommendation.
- *Oversight:* DHS may visit any employer, with 48 hours' notice unless a complaint triggered the visit.
This is the answer to the job-specific question: a startup, a staffing firm, or a small company that is not in E-Verify cannot host a STEM extension, however good the job is. ISSS's pages link to E-Verify's search for participating employers.
## The Application Clocks
1. **Apply early**
	A student files Form I-765 up to 90 days before the current OPT expires, after the DSO recommendation, and within 60 days of the DSO's SEVIS entry.
2. **Stay covered while it is pending**
	A timely and proper filing, with the signed I-983 given to the DSO, extends the old work permit automatically for up to 180 days or until USCIS decides, whichever is first. This sits in the F-1 regulations and in USCIS's OPT page, and the October 8 OPT fee notice restates it, so I read the October 14 end of automatic renewals as separate ([[EAD and OPT Processing]]).
3. **Unemployment**
	An additional 60 days on top of the 90 on initial OPT, for 150 in all.
## Why DHS Is Looking Hard At It
The OPT fee notice gives DHS's numbers. DHS projected in 2016 that STEM OPT would involve almost 50,000 students in year one and about 92,000 by year ten, which is 2026. By 2024 it counted 165,524 STEM OPT participants alongside 194,554 on other OPT. DHS calls that nearly double the 85,000 annual H-1B limit and says the extension has become a channel to avoid H-1B caps ([[OPT Fee Proposal]]).
DHS also says employer attestations on the I-983 are not verified unless there is a site visit, and it describes site visits finding clusters of employers with identical websites and shared personnel. It quotes the presidential proclamation's unemployment rates for recent graduates, 6.1 percent for computer science majors and 7.5 percent for computer engineering, and argues OPT labor adds to that pressure. These are the government's claims, and the notice is a proposal.
## What Is Proposed
- A fee of $30,000 on the school for each OPT recommendation after the first, which includes a STEM extension.
- A fixed-period admission rule, now postponed, that would require an extension-of-stay application to take the extension ([[Duration of Status Rule]]).
## What It Means For Me
- Computer science is on the list, so the extension should be available if the I-20 shows 11.0701 or another listed code.
- The first post-graduation employer should be in E-Verify and willing to sign an I-983, or I must plan to change employers with a new I-983 within 10 days of starting.
- The extension supplies the second and third H-1B registrations, which is my inference from the OPT and registration calendars ([[H-1B Lottery]], [[2028 Graduation Timeline]]).
## Failure Modes
- Using pre-completion OPT until the 12 months are gone ([[Pre-Completion OPT]]).
- An employer not in E-Verify.
- Filing after the current OPT ends, or after 60 days from the DSO entry.
- A new job with no new I-983 within 10 days.
- Hosting at a company that the government may view as a shell, and being caught in a site visit.
