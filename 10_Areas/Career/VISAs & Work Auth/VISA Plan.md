# VISA Plan
This is my dated route from F-1 student to an H-1B job, with work authorization in place at every step. It says what to do, on what date, with what paperwork and money, and where the proof lives. Read it first each week, do only the next row, and open [[VISA Board]] when I need the reason behind a row.
> [!SUCCESS]
> **The whole system in three lines.** No deadline binds me before March 2027, and the March 2027 date applies only if I choose pre-completion OPT. The date that matters most is **January 31 to February 14, 2028**, when I ask for the OPT I-20 and file. Everything before it is preparation, and everything after it is waiting on a clock I have already started.
## Goal
==Be able to start my first job right after graduation with an OPT work permit already approved, proven by filing the application on about February 14, 2028 and getting a decision by about March 27, 2028 with premium processing.==
### Where I Stand
I am a junior at the University of Minnesota studying computer science, in [[F-1 Visa|F-1 status]], finishing in spring 2028. UMN's official calendar ends the Spring 2028 term on **Wednesday, May 10, 2028**, and every OPT date counts from it. The ISSS advisor I met on October 23 confirmed that the I-20 end date is only an estimate and the real one is the end of the semester when all requirements are done ([[ISSS October Meeting Brief]]). Computer Science (CIP code 11.0701) is on the STEM list, so the 24-month extension is possible if my I-20 carries that code, which I am asking ISSS to confirm ([[STEM OPT Extension]]).
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **What the courts did this week, in one box.** On **November 16** a judge cancelled the rule that would have ended duration of status, so I keep open-ended admission and the 60-day windows. On **November 17** a judge stayed the August CPT guidance, and ISSS announced that it resumes both required and elective CPT requests on **Monday, November 23**. Both orders are temporary, because the government can appeal until about January 15 and January 16, 2027 ([[Duration of Status Rule]], [[CPT Lawsuit]]). I plan on the rules in force today and treat any appeal as a change to log, not a reason to worry.

### Track 4: CPT, Now Open
CPT is work my school approves as part of my degree. It uses no OPT months and costs no fee, so it is the best route when open. UMN paused it in August. A court stayed the August guidance on November 17, and ISSS announced that it will resume processing **both required and elective** CPT requests, with the MyISSS form opening **Monday, November 23**. Litigation is ongoing, so I treat CPT as open but reversible ([[CPT Program]], [[CPT Lawsuit]]).
> [!WARNING]
> Do not start any CPT without the new I-20 in hand. Page 2 lists the employer, the address, and the dates, and a new employer needs a new I-20.

I run these steps the week I hold an offer, because ISSS has reopened:
1. Hold a written internship offer in computer science, with exact start and end dates.
2. Take the ISSS CPT online workshop and save the certificate.
3. Meet my academic advisor to confirm the job fits the major.
4. Register for a CPT course of at least one credit, such as an internship reflection course, and get the instructor's signature on the verification form.
5. Submit the CPT request in MyISSS. ISSS takes up to 7 business days and about 2 to 3 weeks in all.
6. Start only after I hold the new I-20.
Part-time CPT is unlimited, but 12 months of full-time CPT ends my OPT eligibility at this degree level.
> [!WARNING]
> A CPT approval is not guaranteed to survive an appeal. ISSS paused CPT in August, so before I plan an internship around CPT alone I ask ISSS in writing what happens to approvals if the order is reversed (Q12b in [[Questions for Next Meeting]]), and I keep pre-completion OPT as the fallback.

### Track 5: Post-Completion OPT
This is the main event, and the one with the most dates. [[OPT Playbook]] holds the full reasoning.
#### Step By Step
1. **By Dec 15, 2027:** finish the online OPT workshop and save the certificate.
2. **By Jan 15, 2028:** meet my academic advisor to confirm the program end date.
3. **Mon Jan 31, 2028:** request the OPT I-20 in MyISSS. This is the earliest day, 100 days before May 10.
4. **Within 10 business days:** ISSS issues the I-20. USCIS must receive my application within 30 days of its issue date.
5. **While I wait,** gather the fee, my electronic I-94, my passport photo page, my past I-20s, and the new I-20. Photos must be unmounted and unretouched, and I stay ready for a biometrics appointment.
6. **About Mon Feb 14, 2028:** file Form I-765 online with premium processing (Form I-907). The earliest USCIS can receive it is Thu Feb 10.
7. **Do not travel until the application is filed.** Leaving during the 60-day period before filing ends that period and my right to apply.
8. **Wait.** A decision with premium comes by about Mar 27, and a normal wait ends about Jul 14. A request for evidence stops and restarts the premium clock, so I tell ISSS at once.
9. **When the card arrives,** check it for mistakes, send ISSS a copy, and start work only when the card is in my hand and its start date has arrived. A requested start date cannot be more than 60 days after May 10.
#### After May 10: What I Do And When
| When | What | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wed May 10, 2028 | Program ends. The 60-day period begins | Every OPT window counts from this date |
| Until the card arrives | Do not work, even if the requested start date has passed | USCIS says a student may not begin OPT until it approves the application and the card arrives |
| When the SEVP Portal email arrives | Create the account within 31 days | The link expires. ISSS will not do it for me |
| Within 10 days of any new job, address, or phone change | Report it in the portal | The portal will not accept a job that started more than 10 days ago |
| Throughout OPT | Work at least 20 hours a week in jobs related to my major | Paid jobs, several jobs, contract work, self-employment, and unpaid or volunteer work all count, so keep written proof |
| Throughout OPT | Stay under 90 days without a qualifying job | 150 with the STEM extension. Days spent outside the U.S. count |
| Always | Do not start a new degree or transfer schools | Either ends the remaining OPT time for good |
> [!TIP]
> Re-entering the U.S. on OPT needs a passport, a valid F-1 visa, an OPT I-20 with a travel signature under 6 months old, the work permit or its receipt, and proof of a job or job offer. Renew the F-1 visa before graduating if I can.
### Track 6: The STEM Extension
It is a second, separate application near the end of post-completion OPT, and I cannot file it at the same time as the first.
1. **Check the CIP code** on my I-20 against the DHS STEM list. Computer Science (11.0701) is on it.
2. **Find an E-Verify employer** who will sign the training plan (Form I-983).
3. **Take the online STEM workshop,** complete the plan with the employer, pay the $400 ISSS fee, and ask ISSS for the STEM I-20.
4. **File with USCIS** no more than 90 days before my current card expires and no later than its expiry date. Include the new I-20, my last card, my passport page, my I-94, my diploma and final transcript, and UMN's accreditation page.
5. **Wait about 3.5 to 4 months.** A timely filing keeps me working up to 180 days or until USCIS decides.
If my card runs to July 9, 2029, ISSS opens Mar 31, 2029 and USCIS opens Apr 10, 2029. I re-derive both from the real end date of my card.
### Track 7: H-1B
==The first registration is in March 2028, before I graduate, so the H-1B plan starts at the interview stage in fall 2027.== The employer registers me at a wage level, and if I am selected the petition is filed from April 1 for an October 1 start. Cap-gap keeps me working between the end of OPT and that start. [[H-1B Odds Plan]] turns the lottery into the choices I control.
- *Employer questions, asked at the interview:* does the company use E-Verify, will it sign a training plan, will it register me for H-1B, and at what wage level.
- *A cap-exempt employer,* such as a university or research lab, files with no lottery but gives no cap-gap.
- *The $100,000 payment:* an in-country change of status is not covered either way ([[H-1B 100K Payment]]).
