## Reading Across The Cases
==Five of these eight entries sit in Massachusetts or the First Circuit, so how that one circuit treats executive action on students and workers sets the practical outcome for most of my questions.== The Presidents' Alliance, NAFSA, and AICUM are plaintiffs in both of the Massachusetts cases on student rules, and the CPT plaintiffs' brief cites Judge Saylor's September 14 order as precedent for the kind of relief it seeks. Jenner & Block represents the CPT plaintiffs and McDermott Will & Schulte represents the duration of status plaintiffs.
The AAU appears twice, as lead plaintiff in the CPT case and as an appellant in the D.C. Circuit $100,000 case. The University of Minnesota is an AAU member, which gives my school a direct institutional link to both ([[CPT Lawsuit]]). Minnesota is also one of the plaintiff states in the California case.
Two pairs of cases are easy to confuse. The CPT case and the duration of status case are different cases before different judges about different documents, and a ruling in one does not decide the other. The same goes for the First Circuit's two appeals, one about duration of status and one about the $100,000 payment.

## What The Rulings Mean
- *Postponement under section 705:* a court delays the effective date of an agency action while a challenge proceeds. This is what happened to the duration of status rule. The rule exists but cannot take effect.
- *Preliminary injunction:* an order barring the government from enforcing something against the plaintiffs, temporarily, based on the likelihood of winning and the harm without it. The docket calls the duration of status order one, and the Global Nurse Force order was granted in part.
- *Summary judgment and vacatur:* a final merits decision. Vacatur sets the agency action aside completely, as in the $100,000 payment guidance on June 8.
- *Administrative stay:* a short pause so a higher court can consider a longer one. The district judge in the California case used one before the First Circuit ruled.
- *Stay pending appeal:* a request that a ruling not take effect while the loser appeals. The First Circuit denied it on July 24.
- *Rule 28(j) letter:* a short notice to an appeals court of new authority. The D.C. Circuit parties have filed three of them.
- *Expedited briefing:* a faster schedule. It does not alter the order under review.
