# CSCI 4521 — Preparation
==Homework (40%) and Quizzes (30%) are 70% of the grade, and the lowest 3 participation activities get dropped automatically - the real risk here isn't a single bad assignment, it's falling behind on the hands-on coding habit the whole course is built around.==
## Where the Points Actually Are
Homework 1-4 (40%) and the Final Project (20%, ~2x a single homework in effort) together are 60% of the grade in coding-and-report form - Quizzes (30%) and Participation (10%) make up the rest. Unlike most other Fall'26 courses, there's no midterm/final exam at all - everything is homework, quizzes, project, and participation.
## Cheapest Points
Participation activities (10%) have a built-in cushion - **lowest 3 dropped** automatically, and they're graded on completion/effort, not correctness. Quizzes allow two pages of notes (front and back), which softens the memorization burden compared to a closed-book exam.

- **Per-assignment AI policy, not a blanket rule** - every assignment gets its own green/yellow/red designation. Using AI on a red-flagged assignment isn't a gray area the way it might be in a course with one policy for everything.
- **In-class activities cannot be made up under any circumstance** - unlike the homework/quiz late-work grace day below, missing a chime-in activity in person loses those points permanently (unless it falls in the dropped-lowest-3).
- **The instructor lectures live by writing code in Google Colab** - per direct observation, the actual teaching format is the professor writing code himself in real time, with the class following along and annotating with comments, rather than pre-built slides carrying the lecture. Prior textbook/resource reading before class matters more here than in a slide-driven course, since class time itself is spent extending that base knowledge live rather than introducing it from scratch - the useful question to ask in class is "what exactly does this do," not "what is this concept," since the concept should already be familiar going in.
## Study Cadence
> [!TIP] The most forgiving late policy of any Fall'26 course
> A full 24-hour, zero-penalty grace day on every assignment, no exceptions process needed - just don't need more than a day. Genuine life circumstances beyond that need instructor contact within 72 hours of being able to do so.
Given the live-coding lecture format, the highest-leverage habit is reading the relevant section of *An Introduction to Statistical Learning* (see [[20_Progress/Degree/CSCI 4521/Textbook/Textbook Map|Textbook Map]]) before each class covering a new topic, not after - lecture time is spent building on that base in real time via Colab, not re-explaining it from zero. Quizzes are biweekly on Thursdays and focus on interpreting results on new datasets rather than recalling definitions, so practice applying each unit's methods to an unfamiliar dataset before its quiz, not just reviewing notes.
