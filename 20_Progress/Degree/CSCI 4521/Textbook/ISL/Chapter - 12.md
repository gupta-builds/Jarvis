# ISL Chapter 12 — Unsupervised Learning, §12.2 PCA & §12.4 Clustering Methods
**Source:** *An Introduction to Statistical Learning, with Applications in Python* (James, Witten, Hastie, Tibshirani, Taylor), Chapter 12, §12.2 and §12.4.1, pp. 504-524.
**Read from:** `D:\_Anant\10_Areas\UMN\Classes\CSCI\CSCI 4521\Textbook & Resources\ISL Textbook CSCI 4521.pdf`.
**Course role:** Week 5's §12.4 reading and Week 6's §12.2 reading support LEC 2.3 (k-means), LEC 2.4 (PCA), and LEC 2.5 — this chapter's treatment is the non-deep-learning-framed companion to [[20_Progress/Degree/CSCI 4521/Textbook/DLB/Chapter - 5|DLB Chapter 5]]'s §5.8, deriving the same two mechanisms from an optimization-and-geometry angle rather than a representation-learning angle.
## Chapter Summary
==PCA finds the low-dimensional linear projection that keeps the most variance (equivalently, lies closest to the data in squared-distance terms); k-means finds the partition into $K$ groups that minimizes total within-cluster squared distance — both are ways of summarizing data with no labels, but they summarize different things.==
*Mechanism:* PCA solves for a sequence of orthogonal loading vectors $\phi_1,\phi_2,\dots$, each one capturing the most remaining variance subject to being uncorrelated with the previous ones; the first $M$ components give the best possible rank-$M$ linear approximation to the data. K-means instead partitions observations into non-overlapping sets $C_1,\dots,C_K$ to minimize $\sum_k W(C_k)$, the total within-cluster sum of squared pairwise distances; because exhaustively checking all $K^n$ partitions is infeasible, the algorithm instead iterates centroid-assignment and reassignment to a local (not global) optimum, so running it from several random initializations and keeping the best result is standard practice.
## Key Concepts
- **Principal component as a direction of maximal variance:** the first principal component $Z_1=\phi_{11}X_1+\cdots+\phi_{p1}X_p$ is the normalized ($\sum_j\phi_{j1}^2=1$) linear combination of the features with the largest sample variance. Each subsequent component maximizes variance subject to being uncorrelated with (equivalently, orthogonal in loading-space to) all previous components.
- **Two equivalent interpretations:** (1) the loading vector defines the direction along which the data vary most, and projecting onto it gives the scores; (2) the first $M$ components together give the best (least-squares) $M$-dimensional linear approximation to the data — these are the same solution viewed two ways, not two different procedures.
- **Clustering vs. PCA, the chapter's own contrast:** "PCA looks to find a low-dimensional representation of the observations that explain a good fraction of the variance; clustering looks to find homogeneous subgroups among the observations." They both compress the data, but toward different goals — do not treat them as interchangeable "dimensionality reduction."
- **Within-cluster variation $W(C_k)$:** defined via squared Euclidean distance, $W(C_k)=\frac{1}{|C_k|}\sum_{i,i'\in C_k}\sum_j (x_{ij}-x_{i'j})^2$ — the sum of all pairwise squared distances inside the cluster, normalized by cluster size. K-means minimizes the total of this across all $K$ clusters.
- **K-means is a local-optimum heuristic, not an exact solver:** the exact optimization over all possible partitions is combinatorially infeasible ($K^n$ partitions), so Algorithm 12.2 (initialize randomly, alternate centroid-computation and reassignment) is only guaranteed to reach *a* local optimum, and a different random initialization can land on a different one — hence the "run several times, keep the best objective value" practice.
## Full Reading Notes
### §12.2 Principal Components Analysis (p. 504)
Motivates PCA as a visualization problem first: with $p$ features there are $\binom{p}{2}$ pairwise scatterplots, which becomes unmanageable for even moderate $p$ (45 plots at $p=10$), so PCA instead finds a small number of "interesting" (high-variance) linear combinations to plot directly. §12.2.1 (p. 505) derives the first component as the solution to $\max_{\phi_{11},\dots,\phi_{p1}} \frac1n\sum_i\left(\sum_j\phi_{j1}x_{ij}\right)^2$ subject to $\sum_j\phi_{j1}^2=1$ (Eq. 12.3) — solved via eigendecomposition (or, equivalently, the SVD, per the chapter's own footnote). The worked USArrests example (p. 507-508, Fig. 12.1, Table 12.1) gives real loadings: PC1 places roughly equal weight on Murder (0.5359), Assault (0.5832 — note: table formatting in the extracted text pairs UrbanPop's row label oddly, cross-check against the rendered figure), and Rape (0.2782/0.5434 depending on alignment), with much less weight on UrbanPop — read as "PC1 ≈ overall violent-crime rate," while PC2 loads heavily on UrbanPop alone ("PC2 ≈ urbanization level"). §12.2.2 (p. 508) gives the alternative "closest line/plane/hyperplane to the data" interpretation, formalized as the minimum-reconstruction-error optimization in Eq. 12.6.
### §12.4 Clustering Methods (p. 520)
Opens with the breast-cancer-subtype and market-segmentation motivating examples, then draws the chapter's own PCA-vs-clustering contrast quoted above. §12.4.1 (p. 521) sets up K-means formally: partition sets $C_1,\dots,C_K$ satisfying (1) their union is all $n$ observations and (2) they're pairwise disjoint; defines $W(C_k)$ via squared Euclidean distance (Eq. 12.16); states the full objective (Eq. 12.17); then gives Algorithm 12.2's two-step loop. The worked toy example (p. 522-524, Figs. 12.7-12.9) uses 150 simulated 2-D points: running K-means six times from six different random initializations at $K=3$ finds three distinct local optima with objective values 320.9, 235.8 (achieved by multiple runs), and 310.9 — 235.8 is the best found and is treated as the answer, even though nothing guarantees it's the true global optimum.
## Worked Example
- USArrests PCA: PC1's loadings put Murder/Assault/Rape all above ~0.53 and UrbanPop near 0.18 — concretely, California/Nevada/Florida score high on PC1 (high crime), North Dakota scores low; California additionally scores high on PC2 (high urbanization), Mississippi the opposite. This is the chapter's own demonstration that a loading vector's *sign pattern and magnitude* is directly interpretable, not just a black-box projection.
- K-means on the 150-point toy dataset: six runs at $K=3$ from different random seeds produced objective values of 320.9, 235.8, 235.8, 235.8, 235.8, and 310.9 — four of six runs converged to the same (best-found) local optimum. This is the textbook's own concrete argument for why "run it multiple times and keep the lowest objective" is standard practice, not caution for its own sake.
