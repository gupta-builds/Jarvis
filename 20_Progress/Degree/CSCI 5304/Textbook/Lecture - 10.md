## Chapter Summary
==Gram-Schmidt is triangular orthogonalization (triangular operations on the right turn $A$ orthogonal); Householder is orthogonal triangularization (orthogonal operations on the left turn $A$ triangular) - the same QR factorization reached from the opposite direction.== *Mechanism:* each $Q_k$ is a Householder reflector $F=I-2vv^*/v^*v$ chosen so that left-multiplying by it zeroes out the entries below the diagonal in column $k$ without disturbing zeros already introduced in earlier columns (eq. 10.1); after $n$ such reflections, $Q_n\cdots Q_2Q_1A=R$ is upper-triangular, and $Q=Q_1^*Q_2^*\cdots Q_n^*$ is unitary, giving the full QR factorization $A=QR$. The result is numerically more stable than Gram-Schmidt, at the cost of losing GS's usefulness as a basis for iterative methods.
## Key Concepts
- **Orthogonal triangularization vs. triangular orthogonalization** (p. 69-70): Gram-Schmidt applies triangular $R_k$'s on the *right* of $A$ to reach orthonormal columns $\hat Q$; Householder applies unitary $Q_k$'s on the *left* of $A$ to reach a triangular $R$ - structurally opposite routes to the same factorization.
- **Zero-introducing $Q_k$** (p. 70, eq. 10.1-10.2): $Q_k=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\0&F\end{bmatrix}$ where $I$ is $(k-1)\times(k-1)$ and $F$ is an $(m-k+1)\times(m-k+1)$ unitary **Householder reflector** - fixes the first $k-1$ columns, introduces zeros below the diagonal in column $k$.
- **Householder reflector** $F=I-2\dfrac{vv^*}{v^*v}$ (p. 71-72, eq. 10.4): reflects $\mathbb C^{m-k+1}$ across the hyperplane $H$ orthogonal to $v=\|x\|e_1-x$, sending $x\mapsto Fx=\|x\|e_1$ (eq. 10.3). Differs from the orthogonal projector $P=I-vv^*/v^*v$ (Lecture 6) only by the factor of 2 - a projection goes halfway, a reflection goes all the way across.
- **The better of two reflectors** (p. 72-73, eq. 10.5): either sign of $z\|x\|e_1$ works algebraically, but numerical stability requires choosing $v=\mathrm{sign}(x_1)\|x\|e_1+x$ (convention $\mathrm{sign}(x_1)=1$ if $x_1=0$) - this reflects $x$ to the *farther* of the two candidate images, so computing $v$ never subtracts two nearly-equal quantities (no cancellation error).
- **Algorithm 10.1** (p. 73-74): for $k=1$ to $n$, extract $x=A_{k:m,k}$, build $v_k=\mathrm{sign}(x_1)\|x\|_2e_1+x$, normalize $v_k=v_k/\|v_k\|_2$, update $A_{k:m,k:n}=A_{k:m,k:n}-2v_k(v_k^*A_{k:m,k:n})$ - works in place on $A$, storing only the $v_k$'s, never forming $Q$ explicitly.
- **Implicit $Q$ application** (p. 74, Algorithms 10.2-10.3): $Q^*b$ is computed by applying the same $v_k$ updates to $b$ in the forward order $k=1,\ldots,n$ (eq. 10.6); $Qx$ is computed by the *same* updates applied in reverse order $k=n,\ldots,1$ (eq. 10.7) - both cost $O(mn)$, not $O(mn^2)$, because $Q$ is never actually formed as a matrix.
- **Operation count** (p. 75-76, eq. 10.9): $\sim 2mn^2-\tfrac23n^3$ flops, derived geometrically (as in Lecture 8) by splitting the solid of work into a ziggurat (volume $\tfrac13n^3$) and a staircase (volume $\tfrac12(m-n)n^2$), then multiplying by 4 flops/entry.
## Full Reading Notes
### Householder and Gram-Schmidt (p. 69-70)
Restates Lecture 8's $A\underbrace{R_1\cdots R_n}_{\hat R^{-1}}=\hat Q$ (triangular orthogonalization) against Householder's $\underbrace{Q_n\cdots Q_1}_{Q^*}A=R$ (orthogonal triangularization) - the two methods reach the same QR factorization from structurally opposite directions, one building $\hat Q$ by triangular operations, the other building $R$ by orthogonal ones.
### Triangularizing by Introducing Zeros (p. 70)
Shown concretely for a $5\times3$ matrix (eq. 10.1): $Q_1$ zeroes column 1's subdiagonal entries (rows 2-5), $Q_2$ zeroes column 2's subdiagonal entries (rows 3-5) without disturbing $Q_1$'s zeros, $Q_3$ zeroes column 3's (rows 4-5). In general $Q_k$ operates only on rows $k,\ldots,m$, and because it forms linear combinations of rows that are already zero in the first $k-1$ columns, those zeros survive.
### Householder Reflectors (p. 70-72)
Each $Q_k$ has block form $\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\0&F\end{bmatrix}$ (eq. 10.2); $F$ must send the subdiagonal portion $x$ of the column to $\|x\|e_1$ (eq. 10.3, Fig. 10.1). $F$ is built as a reflection across the hyperplane $H$ orthogonal to $v=\|x\|e_1-x$: reusing Lecture 6's projector formula $Py=(I-vv^*/v^*v)y$ (eq. 6.11), reflecting instead of projecting means going *twice* as far in the same direction, giving $Fy=(I-2vv^*/v^*v)y$ (eq. 10.4). The projector (rank $m-1$) and the reflector (full rank, unitary) differ only by that factor of 2.
### The Better of Two Reflectors (p. 72-73)
Two hyperplanes $H^+,H^-$ both reflect $x$ to a vector of the form $z\|x\|e_1$ (Fig. 10.2) - mathematically either works, but if the angle between $H^+$ and the $e_1$ axis is small, $v=\|x\|e_1-x$ is much smaller than $x$, so computing it subtracts nearly-equal quantities and suffers cancellation error. Choosing $z=-\mathrm{sign}(x_1)$ gives $v=\mathrm{sign}(x_1)\|x\|e_1+x$ (eq. 10.5), which guarantees $\|v\|$ is never smaller than $\|x\|$ - the stable choice.
### The Algorithm (p. 73-74)
Uses MATLAB-style submatrix notation $A_{i:i',j:j'}$. **Algorithm 10.1**: for $k=1$ to $n$: $x=A_{k:m,k}$; $v_k=\mathrm{sign}(x_1)\|x\|_2e_1+x$; $v_k=v_k/\|v_k\|_2$; $A_{k:m,k:n}=A_{k:m,k:n}-2v_k(v_k^*A_{k:m,k:n})$. Leaves $R$ in place of $A$, storing the $n$ reflection vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ for later use.
### Applying or Forming Q (p. 74-75)
$Q$ or $\hat Q$ is deliberately never constructed explicitly - most uses only need $Q^*b$ or $Qx$. **Algorithm 10.2** ($Q^*b$, eq. 10.6): for $k=1$ to $n$, $b_{k:m}=b_{k:m}-2v_k(v_k^*b_{k:m})$. **Algorithm 10.3** ($Qx$, eq. 10.7): the same update, but for $k=n$ downto $1$ (reverse order, since $Q=Q_1Q_2\cdots Q_n$). Both cost $O(mn)$ rather than $O(mn^2)$. If $Q$ really must be formed, the best way is via Algorithm 10.3 applied to $e_1,\ldots,e_m$, since it starts with the reflections that touch the smallest part of the vector.
### Operation Count (p. 75-76)
The inner-loop update (eq. 10.8) costs $\sim4l$ flops for a vector of length $l=m-k+1$ ($l$ subtract, $l$ scale, $2l-1$ dot product). Summed geometrically (ziggurat + staircase solid, same technique as Lecture 8's Gram-Schmidt count): **Work for Householder: $\sim2mn^2-\tfrac23n^3$ flops** (eq. 10.9) - asymptotically the same leading term as Gram-Schmidt's $2mn^2$, but with a more favorable lower-order correction.
