# Board Role

Role Creation assignment for the [[20_Progress/Degree/ENGL 1004/ENGL 1004 Board|ENGL 1004]] Pillsbury School Board simulation (10/8, 10/13, 10/20), which argues over three challenged titles: *And Tango Makes Three*, *Melissa*, and *The Bluest Eye*. Assigned role bucket: **Community/Volunteer**. The revised role sketch was due by the start of class on 10/1 - written 2026-10-02, one day late unless there's an extension not recorded here. ## Full sketch (typed backup / reference)
### 1. Back story
a. Born and raised in Pillsbury. Left for a library science degree, worked at two other districts for six years, then came home when her mother got sick - meant to stay a year, stayed thirty.
b. Working-class family - her father worked at a hardware store, her mother cleaned houses part-time. Neither parent read much themselves, which she thinks matters: she didn't inherit her love of books from a literary household, she found it on her own.
c. The town librarian let her read above her assigned level once she proved she could handle it. Books are a toolkit to her, not something sacred - thirty years of cataloguing taught her to have real opinions about which books actually work in a classroom and which are just trendy.
d. Catholic upbringing, lapsed; still goes to Christmas Eve Mass for her mother-in-law's sake. Working-class Rust Belt sensibility - instinctively distrusts people who perform their opinions loudly, in either direction.
e. Her second year as Pillsbury Elementary's librarian, a parent complained about a picture book with two dads in it. Her principal asked her to just quietly stop reordering it once the copy wore out. She didn't make it a public fight - she just kept buying a replacement every time one disappeared, for 11 years, until district policy caught up and nobody noticed it had never actually left the shelf.
f. When things don't go her way she goes quiet, not loud - she drafts her actual argument over the next week and comes back with it written out rather than escalating in the room.
g. No political heroes. Her real one is the unbroken string of Pillsbury Elementary librarians going back to the 1960s whose circulation logs she inherited and cross-referenced to see which "controversial" books actually got checked out the most.
h. Volunteers three mornings a week at the Friends of the Library used bookstore, does the crossword, walks her dog (Melville, a joke name from an old coworker), rereads *Little Women* every few years.
i. Closest friends are other retired teachers and librarians, plus the current elementary librarian she mentors informally.
j. Retired Pillsbury Elementary librarian (30 years); now a Friends of the Library volunteer.
k. Wants the district to adopt a real, written reconsideration policy - modeled on what Minn. Stat. 134.51 Subd. 5 actually requires - before she dies, so this fight stops depending entirely on who happens to hold her old job.
### 2. Stance on this issue
a. Enters strongly leaning against removal, but not an absolutist.
b. Thirty years of actual circulation data taught her which books kids sought out quietly and which sat untouched - she thinks the Pico "narrowly partisan" test matches what she already believed from experience. A campaign against *Tango* or *Melissa* has nothing to do with content kids actually encounter and everything to do with a character's identity. *The Bluest Eye* is different: the rape scene is real, and deciding which grade should read it is a legitimate pedagogical/shelving question under Subd. 3, not a viewpoint objection.
c. Firm on *Tango* and *Melissa*. Genuinely open to a shelving compromise on *The Bluest Eye* - keep it in the high school curriculum, move the middle school's copy to independent/library reading rather than a classroom assignment.
### 3. Temperament / Personality
a. Quiet in large groups, not a talker.
b. Motivated by the 11-years-of-quiet-reordering memory - she isn't trying to win an argument in the room, she's trying to make sure the book is still on the shelf in five years regardless of who's on the board by then.
c. Works the room one-on-one before and after meetings rather than grandstanding during them.
d. Not a joker, not a complainer - the one person who actually shows up with printed circulation numbers.
### 4. Strategy
a. Keep *Tango* and *Melissa* on the shelves outright - no real question under Pico/Subd. 1. Get *The Bluest Eye* kept in the high school curriculum specifically, with the middle school's copy moved to independent reading - a shelving compromise, not a removal.
b. Reframes every debate from "is this book appropriate" to "what specific passage, for what grade, are we actually discussing" - the same whole-work-vs-isolated-passage move the course's own case law trains for, and her own thirty-year professional habit.
### 5. Poster exercise
Walking past a "Stop Book Bans!" poster, she slows down to read the whole flyer underneath rather than just reacting to the slogan - irritated that it could mean almost anything on its own, but with the old professional reflex to check whether it actually names a title and a date before deciding what she thinks of it. More likely to photograph it and look the specifics up later than to react on the spot.
