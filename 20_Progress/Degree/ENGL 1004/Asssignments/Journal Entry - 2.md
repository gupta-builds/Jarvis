Content covered: Professor Calgues watching the refugee fleet approach from his terrace and counting the dead through his telescope; the Belgian consulate in Calcutta besieged by parents trying to get children adopted into "paradise," ending in the crowd parading a severely disabled child; the Consul's exchange with a bishop who has thrown in with the migrants; Ballan's "take my son, take my daughter" speech; and the closing chapters on how Western media and officials perform concern about the crisis while doing nothing.

Draft to copy by hand:

I expected the worst part of Camp of the Saints to be a single ugly line I could quote. Instead it's Calgues doing geometry. Watching the fleet through his telescope, the old professor counts the dead like a field scientist: forty circles of bodies per deck, two hundred arms to a circle, eight thousand arms, four thousand people, and that's one deck of one ship. The narration never once steps back from him to say this is monstrous.

That's what makes this book harder to defend than Huckleberry Finn was for Journal 1. Pap's slur worked because Twain wrote Pap as a fool and let the joke land on him. Judge Woolsey's whole-work standard rescued Ulysses the same way: read the book entire and the ugliness resolves into something else. Camp of the Saints doesn't resolve. The Belgian consulate scene, the crowd of mothers holding up a limbless child like a trophy, the coprophage described as barely human, all of it comes straight from the narrator, not from a character the book is mocking. Reading it whole doesn't rescue it. Reading it whole is the problem.

Which makes Minnesota's own statute, the one I leaned on last week, land differently here. Subdivision 1 bans removing a book "based solely on its viewpoint or the messages, ideas, or opinions it conveys." Taken literally, that clause protects this book specifically because its entire argument is the objectionable part. I spent Journal 1 arguing context should save a book from an isolated-passage ban. I didn't expect to find one where the whole context is worse than any single line.
