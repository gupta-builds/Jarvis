### Week 1 - legal foundations (9/8, 9/10)
**United States v. One Book Called "Ulysses,"** 5 F. Supp. 182 (S.D.N.Y. 1933) - found only as a citation, not a local file; full opinion cross-checked by Journal 1 against [law.justia.com](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/5/182/2250768/). Judge Woolsey's holding: the test isn't whether an isolated passage could corrupt the most susceptible reader (the English *Hicklin* rule), it's whether the book taken as a whole, read by a person with average sex instincts, has the effect of stirring the sex impulses or leading to sexually impure thoughts - and whether *Ulysses* was written with pornographic intent or with "a sincerity and honesty of purpose." Captured in `ENGL 1004/Lecture/9_10_26.pdf`.

**Minnesota Session Laws 2024, Chapter 109, Art. 7, Sec. 2 (codified as Minn. Stat. 134.51)** - read in full from `ENGL 1004/Readings/Minnesota Law.pdf`. Full subdivision breakdown:
- Subd. 1 - **Book banning prohibited.** A public library "must not ban, remove, or otherwise restrict access to a book or other material based solely on its viewpoint or the messages, ideas, or opinions it conveys." (The clause Journals 1 and 2 both leaned on.)
- Subd. 2 - Definitions: "public library" covers city/county libraries, joint city-school libraries, school/charter library media centers, and public higher-ed libraries.
- Subd. 3 - **Limitations** (the carve-out Journal 1 cited): nothing in the section stops a library from declining/removing a book for (1) practical reasons - shelf space, damage, obsolescence; (2) "legitimate pedagogical concerns, including but not limited to the appropriateness of potentially sensitive topics for the library's intended audience ... or the likelihood of causing a material and substantial disruption"; or (3) compliance with other state/federal law. Subd. 3(b) also preserves parent/guardian rights under Minn. Stat. 120B.20.
- Subd. 4 - Collection management: a library can't discipline or discriminate against an employee for complying with this section.
- Subd. 5 - **Library materials policy**: every governing body must adopt a written selection/challenge/reconsideration policy, administered only by a licensed library media specialist or someone with a library-science credential, and must report every completed challenge to the Commissioner of Education (title/author, hearing date and minutes, outcome, contact person).
- **Minn. Stat. 120B.20 (Parental Curriculum Review)**, included on the same PDF: every district must let a parent/guardian/adult student review instructional materials and arrange alternative instruction if they object - the district isn't required to pay for it, and a student can't be penalized academically just for using it.

**Florida statutes** - read from `ENGL 1004/Readings/Florida laws.pdf`. This file is a four-statute *summary*, not the verbatim code text, so any claim about exact phrase-for-phrase overlap with Minnesota's language (Journal 1 called the two "nearly identical") can't be fully confirmed from this file alone - flagging that as unverified, not wrong.
- Fla. Stat. § 1006.28 - school boards must adopt library media center selection/removal procedures, publish reading lists online, and give parents/county residents a formal objection process.
- Fla. Stat. § 1006.31 - substantive standard for state-adopted instructional materials: accurate, objective, balanced, noninflammatory, free of pornography/§847.012 material, age-appropriate.
- Fla. Stat. § 1003.42 - required curriculum content (civics, Holocaust history, Black/Hispanic/women's history, etc.); reproductive-health materials need separate public-meeting approval.
- Fla. Stat. § 1001.42 - the "Parental Rights in Education" provisions restricting classroom instruction on sexual orientation/gender identity, with parental-notification and objection-resolution rules.

### Week 2 - Huckleberry Finn ch. 1-6 / Camp of the Saints ch. I-II (9/15, 9/17)
**Adventures of Huckleberry Finn, ch. 1-6 (Mark Twain)** - **no PDF or text file exists in the synced folder**; it's one of the 8 required physical texts, so it was presumably read from the physical book, not a class file. The summary below is outside general knowledge of the public-domain novel, not from an assigned file - verify against your own copy before relying on it for anything graded.
- Ch. 1: Huck narrates, recapping Tom Sawyer's ending; living with Widow Douglas and Miss Watson being "sivilized"; Tom Sawyer's gang forms.
- Ch. 2: the gang's mock-robber-band initiation; Jim (Miss Watson's enslaved man) nearly catches them sneaking out.
- Ch. 3: Huck sours on Tom's games once the "A-rabs" turn out to be a Sunday school picnic; news that Pap's apparent drowned body was found (later revealed to be someone else).
- Ch. 4: Huck's $6,000 from the *Tom Sawyer* treasure sits with Judge Thatcher; Pap returns to town; Huck finds a boot print with a cross in the heel (a hoodoo ward) and consults Jim's hairball oracle.
- Ch. 5: Pap confronts Huck about being "educated," resents Huck acting "better" than him, and fights Judge Thatcher over guardianship.
- Ch. 6: Pap's tirade about a free Black professor from Ohio who can vote and dress well - the scene [[20_Progress/Degree/ENGL 1004/Asssignments/Journal Entry - 1|Journal 1]] built its whole argument around; Pap kidnaps Huck to an isolated cabin, drinks, beats him, and has a DTs hallucination chasing Huck with a knife as the "Angel of Death."

**The team's Utah research note** (`ENGL 1004/Journal Entries/Banned Books in Utah.pdf`) - read in full. Key detail beyond what Journal 1 cited:
- HB29 (2024, amending HB374/2022) applies only to K-12 public/charter school libraries and classrooms, **not public libraries**.
- The statutory trigger is "objectively sensitive material," defined by cross-reference to Utah's pornography/indecency statutes (Utah Code 76-10-1201, -1203, -1227) and the general obscenity test in 32B-1-504 (prurient interest + patently offensive sexual content + lacks serious literary/artistic/political/scientific value, taken as a whole).
- 3 districts (or 2 districts + 5 charter schools) flagging the same book triggers an automatic statewide ban; ~37 titles were on the list as of early 2026, including, notably, ***The Bluest Eye*** by Toni Morrison - the same book assigned for Week 4 here.
- No criminal penalty exists (a 2024 bill, HB417, would have made it a class A misdemeanor; it died in committee). The real teeth are licensing discipline under R277-217-2.
- The note's own conclusion for part 4 (applying Utah law to *Huck Finn* and *Camp of the Saints*) is worth flagging against Journal 1's argument: the research team concluded that **racist language and offensive ideas alone do not meet Utah's statutory "sensitive material" definition**, which is keyed to pornography/indecency specifically - meaning Pap's chapter 6 tirade likely would *not*, by itself, trigger an automatic HB29 ban the way Journal 1's draft implies. Both books would instead go through Utah's ordinary discretionary review process rather than an automatic one. Worth reconciling before Journal 1 is actually submitted, if it hasn't been yet.

**The Camp of the Saints, ch. I-II** (Jean Raspail, Fitzgerald translation) - `ENGL 1004/Readings/from The Camp of the Saints.pdf`, pp. 51-61.
- Ch. I (pp. 51-54): Professor Calgues on his terrace, telescope trained on the refugee fleet run aground below; his "geometry of the dead" calculation - 40 circles of bodies per deck x 200 arms per circle = 8,000 arms = roughly 4,000 people, for one deck of one ship alone.
- Ch. II (pp. 58-61): a young man (armed, self-described as of mixed/immigrant heritage, contemptuous of French heritage and Calgues's "kind") confronts Calgues on the terrace at night; a long, bitter dialogue about inheritance, belief, and whose world it will be; Calgues shoots him.

**Island Trees Union Free School District v. Pico (1982)** - `ENGL 1004/Lecture/9_22-24.pdf`, read in full. Splintered 5-4 ruling, 7 of 9 justices wrote separately.
- Facts: the school board ordered certain books removed as "anti-American, anti-Christian, anti-Semitic, and just plain filthy." Trial court granted summary judgment for the board; the court of appeals reversed, finding a genuine factual dispute over whether the board acted to remove vulgarity (permissible) or to suppress ideas (impermissible).
- Question presented: does removing books from junior high/high school libraries based on content violate the First Amendment?
- Brennan and Marshall (affirm): the "special characteristics of the school library" mean removal implicates students' First Amendment rights; officials may not remove books on "narrowly partisan or political" grounds - that would be "official suppression of ideas."
- Blackmun (affirm, narrower): rejected treating the library as distinct from the school and rejected any student "right to receive" in a public school context, but agreed removal is unconstitutional when its *purpose* is restricting access to political ideas or social perspectives out of simple disapproval.
- White (affirm the judgment only): expressly declined to reach the First Amendment question at all, preferring to let the factual dispute get resolved at trial first.
- Dissent: removal implicates no First Amendment right because (1) the books remained available elsewhere, (2) no "right to receive information" exists in a school setting, (3) courts should defer educational policy to locally accountable officials.

### Week 4 - The Bluest Eye / And Tango Makes Three (9/29, 10/1)
Both are also two of the three titles the simulation itself argues over (the third is *Melissa*), per [[20_Progress/Degree/ENGL 1004/ENGL 1004 Board|the Board note]]'s description of the 10/8-10/20 school-board simulation. **Neither has a file in the synced source folder** - both summaries below are outside general knowledge, not from an assigned file.
> [!WARNING] Not sourced from class files; also not yet actually read this round
- **The Bluest Eye (Toni Morrison, 1970)**: Pecola Breedlove, a Black girl in 1940s Lorain, Ohio, longs for blue eyes as a stand-in for being seen as beautiful/worthy under a white beauty standard that has poisoned how her own family and community see themselves. The novel includes Pecola being raped by her father, Cholly, and becoming pregnant - the specific content that gets it challenged, almost always for "sexually explicit" material and incest, despite its literary standing (Morrison won the Nobel Prize in Literature, 1993). It's on Utah's own 37-title statewide banned list read above - a direct cross-reference worth using in the Role Creation sketch.
- **And Tango Makes Three (Justin Richardson & Peter Parnell, 2005)**: a picture book based on a true story at New York's Central Park Zoo - two male chinstrap penguins, Roy and Silo, pair-bond and are given an egg to hatch, raising the chick (Tango) together. Consistently one of the ALA's most-challenged books for over a decade, almost always challenged on "homosexuality" / "anti-family" / "unsuited for age group" grounds rather than any explicit content at all - the elementary-school equivalent of the Pico "narrowly partisan" problem Journal 3 raises about *Melissa*.
