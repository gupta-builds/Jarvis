### Week 1 (9/8, 9/10)
9/8 was Introduction only, no reading. 9/10's lecture (`ENGL 1004/Lecture/9_10_26.pdf`) covered the Hicklin Test vs. Judge Woolsey's whole-work standard from *United States v. One Book Called "Ulysses,"* 5 F. Supp. 182 (S.D.N.Y. 1933) - full opinion cross-checked against [law.justia.com](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/5/182/2250768/). No Journal Entry was due this week; the Woolsey/Hicklin distinction became the throughline that Journal 1 applied to Huckleberry Finn the following week.
### Week 2 (9/15, 9/17)
9/15's lecture (`ENGL 1004/Lecture/9_15-7_2026.pdf`) covered degrees of "banning," PEN America's definition, and Minnesota's 2024 library-access law. The reading was *Huckleberry Finn* ch. 1-6 (Mark Twain) - [[20_Progress/Degree/ENGL 1004/Asssignments/Journal Entry - 1|Journal 1]] grounded its argument in ch. 6's scene of Pap ranting about a free Black professor in Ohio. Supporting research for that entry: the team's Utah HB29/HB374 note (`ENGL 1004/Journal Entries/Banned Books in Utah.pdf` - the 37-title statewide list, the 3-district automatic-ban trigger, licensing-discipline risk rather than a criminal penalty) and the statute PDFs directly: `Minnesota Law.pdf` (Minn. Stat. 134.51, Subd. 3's pedagogical-concerns carve-out) and `Florida laws.pdf` (the "don't say gay" bill's near-identical phrasing). 9/17 assigned *The Camp of the Saints* ch. 1-3, 6, 11 [Canvas] - read as part of the single capture logged under Week 3 below, since the synced PDF bundles both weeks' chapters together.
### Week 3 (9/22, 9/24)
Reading: `ENGL 1004/Readings/from The Camp of the Saints.pdf` (Jean Raspail, Fitzgerald translation), 15 pages, pp. 51-77 and 98-103, covering chapters I-III, V-VII, and XIV-XV as numbered in the book - this one file covers both the 9/17 assignment (ch. 1-3, 6, 11) and the 9/22 assignment (ch. 13, 19). Content: Professor Calgues counting the dead through his telescope from his terrace; the Belgian consulate in Calcutta besieged by parents trying to get children adopted into "paradise," ending in a crowd parading a severely disabled child; the Consul's exchange with a bishop who has thrown in with the migrants; Ballan's "take my son, take my daughter" speech; and the closing chapters on how Western media and officials perform concern while doing nothing. [[20_Progress/Degree/ENGL 1004/Asssignments/Journal Entry - 2|Journal 2]] grounded its argument in the Calgues "geometry of the dead" opening and tested it against Minn. Stat. 134.51 Subd. 1's viewpoint-based ban prohibition.

In class 9/22 and continuing 9/24: *Island Trees Union Free School District v. Pico* (1982), captured in `ENGL 1004/Lecture/9_22-24.pdf` - the splintered 5-4 ruling (7 of 9 justices wrote separately), Brennan and Marshall's "special characteristics of the school library" standard (removal for "narrowly partisan or political" motives is impermissible "official suppression of ideas"), Blackmun's narrower purpose-based test (unconstitutional only when the purpose is disapproving the idea itself), and the dissent's three-part rejection (the books were available elsewhere, no First Amendment "right to receive" applies in a school, and federal courts should defer to locally accountable officials).

9/24 reading assignment: *Melissa* (Alex Gino).
