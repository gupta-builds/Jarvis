### Draft
**Reflection on Group Idea Pitches**
Anant Gupta | MGMT 3015

**General Strengths**
A strength in nearly every pitch was that they were well planned. All the groups had written down notes on what they were going to say, who was saying what and had prepared answers to common questions. This was evident in their pitched as they worked well together and had a good team effort compared to other classes I have been on. We had an appendix of common questions at the end of our deck and the other groups had clearly prepared similar things. As mentioned in the textbook, it's the research and practice that goes into your slides that makes them valuable, not the slides themselves.

**Most Innovative Pitch**
AtheleMatch stood out because they provided evidence. They showed us their prototype. Showing a prototype means that you can take your founder assumptions and provide something tangible for customers to respond to. By providing a prototype we were able to see what the product was like. It was also the best presentation. It had a logical flow, good communication and the questions kept me engaged throughout. This is what a pitch should be.

**Major Weaknesses**
Overall the main issue was that we were trying to do too much. Our group included. We provided estimates for our costs, our marketing strategy and income forecasts. Our PinBall deck has footnotes mentioning “Costs of $68 for kit = Team planning estimate. 40,000 kits and ~$7M revenue in year 3 = Planning target.” This is in contrast to the Gravyty case study in the textbook where the founders used a bottom-up approach and got their first stranger to pay full price. These tests are in our roadmap as our first stage but weren't included in our presentation.

Another trend was that most pitches had plans to create an app. As mentioned in the textbook, launching another generic mobile app usually fails as they don't have a sustainable competitive advantage. From being a computer science major I can see how easy it is to create apps, meaning there are infinite options for users. Your pitch should explain why people would want to download your app.

**Lessons Learned**
The main thing I have learnt is that I have a problem with my verbal delivery. I get stuck for words, use too many ums and don't flow well. Although I believe in our idea and our team communicate extremely well, this shows that my communication skills are lacking. Seeing AtheleMatch show how much having a clear delivery helped their great idea. Tips from the lecture include keeping it natural, having smooth transitions and finishing on time. I will create three key points to help develop these habits. I will not learn from memory but will speak through my practice, timing myself. I will record myself and note how many fillers I have. I will practise changing each filler into a pause. I also learnt to differentiate between estimates and evidence. For my final presentation I will ask my group to make a note of whether each number is a test or assumption. I will also ask for at least one real customer test.
