# Lecture - 3 — Session 3: The Entrepreneurial Personality (Wed 9/16)
Source: real slide deck `2026 S3015 S3 Entrepreneurial Persoanlity.pptx` (25 slides), read in full 2026-09-21. Reading: Ch 2 continued, per [[20_Progress/Degree/MGMT 3015/Textbook/Textbook Map|Textbook Map]].
## What you must be able to do
- [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 1 & 2]]
- Take the 7-question personality quiz and correctly classify your own dominant type (Explorer, Architect, Connector, Steward) plus its named risk.
- List the six named entrepreneurial attributes (determination, motivation drivers, energy, desire for financial success, need to achieve, independence, support network) and give one line on why each matters.
- Explain why "necessary but insufficient" is the right way to describe entrepreneurial personality, not "sufficient on its own."
- State the four sub-components of "what motivates entrepreneurs" (need for achievement, need for recognition, self-efficacy, locus of control) without collapsing them into one idea.
## Key ideas (short)
- Entrepreneurial personality is **necessary but insufficient** — a real, explicit callout on the slide, not a throwaway line. Having the traits doesn't guarantee success; lacking them makes success much harder.
- The **quiz's four types** (Explorer, Architect, Connector, Steward) aren't ranked — each has a named strength and a named risk, so the quiz isn't sorting "good vs. bad" entrepreneurs, it's sorting *styles*.
- **Determination** gets singled out as the single most-emphasized attribute on the slide, backed by two separate Steve Jobs quotes — perseverance, not raw talent, is framed as the differentiator.
- Motivation itself splits into four distinct drivers (achievement, recognition, self-efficacy, locus of control) — conflating them loses the point that these can pull in different directions for the same person.

## Examples worth keeping
- **The 7-question personality quiz** (Slides 5-11), each with four lettered responses mapped consistently to a type across all seven questions: **A = Explorer** (act first, adjust as you learn), **B = Architect** (analyze, reduce uncertainty through data), **C = Connector** (share risk, mobilize networks), **D = Steward** (wait for favorable odds, respect boundaries).

Mostly C = "The Connector — builds momentum through networks/trust, risk: over-reliance on others." Mostly D = "The Steward — emphasizes stability/discipline/sustainability, well-suited for scaling/governance/family business, risk: missing transformative opportunities."
- Two Steve Jobs quotes used back to back: on determination — "I'm convinced that about half of what separates the successful entrepreneurs from the non-successful ones is pure perseverance"; on achievement — "Being the richest man in the cemetery doesn't matter to me... going to bed at night saying we've done something wonderful... that's what matters to me."
## Lecture
### 1. Agenda
Personality quiz, entrepreneurial attributes, discussion of the Profile assignment — tagged to Chapter 2.
### 2. Entrepreneurial personality quiz (7 questions)
Full question set, each testing a different decision point: (Q2) first reaction to a frustrating problem, (Q3) comfort with uncertainty, (Q4) how you treat failure, (Q5) behavior under scarce resources, (Q6) what you focus on when pitching, (Q7) attitude toward rules/institutions, (Q8) what energizes you most. See Examples above for the full A/B/C/D → type mapping and scoring key.
### 3. Entrepreneurial personality, framed
Single-word slide title, followed immediately by the speaker-note framing: "necessary but insufficient" — the personality types above matter, but the deck pivots straight into naming personality's limits before listing the traits that supposedly define it.
### 4. Has determination
Presented as one of the most important characteristics, with the Jobs perseverance quote (see Examples) and a direct challenge: "Do you have 'it' in you?" — this rhetorical question repeats across three later slides (Energy, Financial Success), functioning as the deck's recurring self-check device.
### 5. What motivates entrepreneurs? (four factors)
Posed as an open question, then answered on the next slide with four named attributes: **need for achievement**, **need for recognition**, **self-efficacy**, **locus of control**. No elaboration given per-factor in the deck — these read as headline terms the textbook chapter is expected to define in depth.
### 6. Is energetic
Requires long work hours; frequently unable to control the number of hours; must respond to the job's demands. Same "do you have 'it' in you?" close.
### 7. Wants financial success
Duplicated across two consecutive slides (14 and 15 of this section) — same content both times: financial success as a primary reason most entrepreneurs cite for going into business, same "do you have 'it' in you?" close. Likely a slide-deck duplication artifact rather than intentional repetition; flagged, not silently deduplicated.
### 8. Has a need to achieve
Entrepreneurs strive to excel and set quite high objectives — willingness to set high goals and enjoy striving toward them. Backed by the second Jobs quote (see Examples), which reframes achievement away from wealth and toward the act of building something.
### 9. Seeks independence
Likes being independent and in control of situations, drawn to trying new things — the shortest of the attribute slides, no quote or elaboration.
### 10. Has a support network
A successful entrepreneur needs a support network — the more positive support from family/friends, the more the entrepreneur can concentrate on making the business succeed. Framed as an external attribute (who surrounds you) rather than an internal trait, distinct from the other five.
### 11. Integration — closing discussion questions
Two open prompts with no slide answer: "What do these entrepreneurs have in common?" and "Are there negative attributes for entrepreneurial personality? If so, what?" — the second question is a real pivot, since every attribute up to this point was framed positively.

## Lecture-to-textbook synthesis
==Entrepreneurial personality is necessary but insufficient: the course names six real attributes (determination, motivation, energy, drive for financial success, need to achieve, independence, support network), but frames every one of them as a starting condition, not a guarantee.==
*Mechanism:* The "necessary but insufficient" framing does real work — it explains why the course spends far more time later on process (opportunity evaluation, business models, financing) than on personality. Personality gets one session; execution gets the rest of the semester. The four-type quiz reinforces this by refusing to rank styles — Explorer, Architect, Connector, and Steward are all viable, each with a named failure mode, which only makes sense if personality is a starting style, not a success predictor.
- Lecture example/scenario: the two Steve Jobs quotes are doing different jobs — the determination quote argues perseverance beats talent, the achievement quote argues purpose beats wealth. Together they push back against "wants financial success" being the deck's own primary framing two slides earlier — a real internal tension in the source, not resolved by the deck itself.
- Textbook connection: [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 1 & 2]] — "Ingredients for a Successful New Business" should be where the book gives the fuller, referenced version of these six attributes.
