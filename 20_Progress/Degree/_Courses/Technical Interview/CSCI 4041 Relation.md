# CSCI 4041 Relation — TIP103 Revision Map
==TIP103 sets the pace; this folder's completed Spring'26 CSCI 4041 material is what gets revised one unit ahead of it.==
[[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/Technical Interview|Technical Interview]] (CodePath TIP103) supplies problems and a schedule but no textbook of its own. This note maps each of TIP103's nine syllabus topics onto the matching week, textbook chapter, and concept note already written for the completed Spring'26 [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/CSCI 4041 Board|CSCI 4041]] course, so a revision pass has something concrete to stand on before the LeetCode/HackerRank problem-solving starts. It maps what already exists — it does not restate TIP103's own schedule, which stays in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/Technical Interview|Technical Interview]]. [[DSA|DSA]] already carries a week-by-week skeleton (`## LeetCode / Weekly Plan`, Week 1&2 through Week 15) that this map builds directly on top of.
> [!WARNING]
> Two notes share the exact name "Introduction to Algorithms": [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Introduction to Algorithms|Concepts' shorter version]] (Parts 1-5 only) and [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Introduction to Algorithms|Textbook's fuller version]] (Parts 1-7 plus the Appendix, `status: tree`). Always link the full path — a bare `[[Introduction to Algorithms]]` is ambiguous between them.
## 1. Technical Interview Skills — UMPIRE, Communication, Big O
UMPIRE (Understand, Match, Plan, Implement, Review, Evaluate) is CodePath's own framework and has no CSCI 4041 equivalent — nothing in this folder covers interview communication strategy, so that stays pure CodePath-portal material, with [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/Preparation & Sources|Preparation & Sources]] as the natural place to capture it once written. The course opens with [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 1 & 2|Week - 1 & 2]]'s [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 1 & 2#Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 - Insertion Sort, Cost Models, and Merge Sort|Chapter 1 & 2 lecture]] and textbook [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 1 & 2#The Role of Algorithms in Computing|Chapter 1: The Role of Algorithms]] and [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 1 & 2#2.2 Analyzing Algorithms|Chapter 2.2: Analyzing Algorithms]], with concept note [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Algorithms/Sorting Algorithms#Definition|Sorting Algorithms]] using insertion/merge sort as the running complexity example. Big O proper falls out of [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 3|Week - 3]]'s [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 3#Chapter - 3 Asymptotic analysis|Chapter 3: Asymptotic Analysis]] section, backed by textbook [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 3 & 4#3.1 O - notation, Ω - notation, and Θ notation|Chapter 3.1: O-notation, Ω-notation, and Θ-notation]] and distilled in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Time Complexity#Definition|Time Complexity]]'s definition and [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Time Complexity#Asymptotic Notation|Asymptotic Notation]] foundation box.
## 2. Core Data Structures — Strings, Linked Lists, Dictionaries
Linked lists and elementary structures were taught in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 4|Week - 4]]'s [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 4#Chapter 7 and Chapter 10 - Quicksort, Partitioning, and Elementary Data Structures|Chapter 7 & 10 lecture]], with the textbook side in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 7 & 10#10.2 Linked Lists|Chapter 10.2: Linked Lists]] and the concept distillation in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Data Structures & Methods/Elementary Data Structures#Linked Lists|Elementary Data Structures: Linked Lists]]. The same week's quicksort/partitioning half is distilled in concept note [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Algorithms/QuickSort#Definition|QuickSort]]. Strings have no dedicated CSCI 4041 note — this course treated arrays/strings as a [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 1 & 2#2.1 Insertion Sort|Chapter 2.1 warm-up]] rather than its own topic, so sliding-window and two-pointer string practice is LeetCode-only revision with no note to lean on. Dictionaries map onto hashing, covered under Topic 3 below.
## 3. Hash Tables & Heaps
Hashing was taught in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 8|Week - 8]]'s [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 8#Chapter 11 - Hash Tables, Collision Handling, and Homework Variants|Chapter 11 lecture]], textbook [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 11#11.2 Hash Tables|Chapter 11.2: Hash Tables]], and distilled in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Data Structures & Methods/Hashing#Definition|Hashing]]'s definition plus its [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Data Structures & Methods/Hashing#Load-Factor Checklist|load-factor]] and [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Data Structures & Methods/Hashing#Open-Addressing Deletion Checklist|open-addressing deletion]] checklists. Heaps came a week earlier, in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 5|Week - 5]]'s [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 5#Chapter 6 and Chapter 12 - Heaps, Priority Queues, and BST Operations|Chapter 6 & 12 lecture]], textbook [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 6 & 12#6.1 Heaps|Chapter 6.1: Heaps]] and [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 6 & 12#6.5 Priority Queues|6.5: Priority Queues]], concept note [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Algorithms/HeapSort#Definition|HeapSort]].

Recursion mechanics come from [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 3|Week - 3]]'s [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 3#Chapter - 4 Divide & Conquer Algorithms (DnC)|Chapter 4: Divide & Conquer]] section and textbook [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 3 & 4#4.0 Introduction to Divide-and-Conquer and Recurrences|Chapter 4.0: Introduction to Recurrences]], concept note [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Algorithms/Divide and Conquer#Definition|Divide and Conquer]]. ## 6. Trees — Binary Trees, BSTs, Traversals
BST operations were taught alongside heaps in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 5|Week - 5]]; textbook [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 6 & 12#12.1 What is a Binary Search Tree?|Chapter 12.1: What is a Binary Search Tree?]], concept note [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Data Structures & Methods/Elementary Data Structures#BST Node Structure and Traversals|Elementary Data Structures: BST Node Structure and Traversals]] and its [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Data Structures & Methods/Elementary Data Structures#Transplant|Transplant]] helper for deletion. General rooted-tree representation is in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 7 & 10#10.3 Representing Rooted Trees|Chapter 10.3: Representing Rooted Trees]] from the same [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 4|Week - 4]] lecture as Topic 2. Balanced-tree depth beyond what TIP103 asks for sits in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 6|Week - 6]] and [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 7|Week - 7]]'s red-black-tree/B-tree/AVL-validation lectures, textbook [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 13#13.1 Properties of Red-Black Trees|Chapter 13: Red-Black Trees]] and [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 18#18.1 Definition of B-Trees|Chapter 18: B-Trees]], concept notes [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Trees/AVL Trees#Definition|AVL Trees]] and [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Trees/B-Trees#Definition|B-Trees]] — worth a pass if extra practice is useful, since Chapter 13's rotation machinery is exactly what the AVL notes reuse.
## 7. Graphs — Representations, BFS, DFS, Topological Sort, Dijkstra's, Union-Find
Representations, BFS, and DFS come from [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 11|Week - 11]]'s [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 11#Chapter 20 - Graph Representations, BFS, and DFS|Chapter 20 lecture]], textbook [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 20#20.1 Representations of Graphs|20.1: Representations]], [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 20#20.2 Breadth-First Search (BFS)|20.2: BFS]], [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 20#20.3 Depth-First Search (DFS)|20.3: DFS]], concept note [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Graphs/Graph Algorithms#Definition|Graph Algorithms]]. Topological sort follows in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 12|Week - 12]]'s [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 12#Chapter 20 - Topological Sort and Strongly Connected Components|Chapter 20 lecture]] and textbook [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 20#20.4 Topological Sort|20.4: Topological Sort]]. Dijkstra's is in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 13|Week - 13]]'s [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 13#Chapter 22 and Chapter 23 - Shortest Paths (Single-Source and All-Pairs)|Chapter 22 & 23 lecture]], textbook [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 22#22.3 Dijkstra's Algorithm|22.3: Dijkstra's Algorithm]], concept note [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Graphs/Shortest Paths#Dijkstra (Ch 22.3)|Shortest Paths: Dijkstra]]. All-pairs shortest paths (Floyd-Warshall) is the same week's other half, textbook [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 23#23.2 The Floyd-Warshall Algorithm|Chapter 23.2: Floyd-Warshall]], concept note [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Graphs/Shortest Paths#Floyd-Warshall (Ch 23.2)|Shortest Paths: Floyd-Warshall]] — past what TIP103 asks for, but the same relaxation framework.
> [!WARNING]
> Union-find has no note anywhere in this folder. [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Graphs/Minimum Spanning Trees#Kruskal|Minimum Spanning Trees: Kruskal]] uses a disjoint-set structure without ever naming or explaining it as its own topic. This is the one Topic 7 subtopic with zero head start from existing notes — treat it as pure LeetCode-from-scratch, and it's the strongest candidate for a new concept note in the next build pass on this folder.
## 8. Dynamic Programming — Memoization, Tabulation, Bottom-Up, Top-Down, Backtracking
[[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 9|Week - 9]] covers this directly — textbook [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 14#14.3 Elements of Dynamic Programming|Chapter 14.3: Elements of Dynamic Programming]], concept note [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Algorithms/Dynamic Programming#Definition|Dynamic Programming]] with its [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Algorithms/Dynamic Programming#DP Design Checklist|DP design checklist]] and [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Algorithms/Dynamic Programming#3. > [!WARNING]
> Backtracking-flavored DP (TIP103's own phrasing: "dynamic programming with backtracking") is only weakly present — CSCI 4041's DP unit covers memoization, tabulation, and bottom-up/top-down directly, but never frames a problem as explicit backtracking-with-memo. Bridge this with LeetCode problems tagged both backtracking and DP rather than expecting a matching note here.

[[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 10|Week - 10]]'s [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 10#Chapter 15 - Greedy Algorithms and Huffman Coding|Chapter 15 lecture]] covers this, textbook [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 15#15.1 An Activity-Selection Problem|Chapter 15.1: Activity Selection]] and [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 15#15.3 Huffman Codes|15.3: Huffman Codes]], concept note [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Algorithms/Greedy Algorithms#Definition|Greedy Algorithms]]. [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Concepts/Graphs/Minimum Spanning Trees#Definition|Minimum Spanning Trees]] (Kruskal/Prim), taught in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Weekly/Week - 12|Week - 12]] and textbook [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Textbook/Chapter - 21#21.2 The Algorithms of Kruskal and Prim|Chapter 21.2: Kruskal and Prim]], is the natural bridge between greedy reasoning and graphs even though TIP103 never names MST directly — worth a revision pass here for exchange-argument practice.
