# Problems Solver — TIP103 Problem-Solving Plan
==Every day, 7 problems minimum: 5 from LeetCode (3 hard, 1 easy, 1 medium) and 2 from real interview-question resources - this is the generic plan for the whole course, not a weekly document.==
This is the course-wide map of what gets solved and where it comes from, till the end of TIP103. [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Problems/Weekly Set|Weekly Set]] pulls a specific week's problems from this same pool; [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Problems/How to Solve Problems|How to Solve Problems]] governs how each one actually gets solved once picked. This note doesn't repeat either — it's the resource map and the daily rule they both draw on.
## The Daily Rule
At least **7 problems solved every day**: 5 from LeetCode (3 hard, 1 easy, 1 medium) plus 2 from interview-question resources outside LeetCode's own problem set. The 3-hard/1-easy/1-medium split on the LeetCode side is deliberate — hard problems build real depth, the easy and medium keep the daily habit from stalling on a single hard problem that won't yield. This is the number to hold the course to; the earlier looser "20+ problems over about 4 days" estimate in [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041 Relation|CSCI 4041 Relation]]'s Status table is superseded by this daily rule and should be read as roughly what 3 days at this rate already produces.
## The Easy/Medium Backbone — Two Fixed Sources, Nothing Else
Locked 2026-10-04 after a direct competitor search (web + vault) turned up nothing better: **only** [[60_Claude/10_Source_Summaries/Github Ingestion/Jobs Starred/tech-interview-handbook|Tech Interview Handbook]]'s [Grind 75](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/grind75/) and **the Pareto Problem Set** ([amanmanazir.com/leetcode](https://www.amanmanazir.com/leetcode), curator "veganaiZe," 49 fixed questions) supply the easy/medium pool — target is 100+ problems across the 15 weeks. The Pareto set is NeetCode-roadmap-shaped (Arrays & Hashing through Graphs, 18 easy/31 medium/0 hard) and is already placed by topic into [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Problems/Detailed Problems List|Detailed Problems List]]'s Weeks 3, 4, 5, 8, 11, and 12. Where the Pareto set doesn't cover a week's topic (Weeks 1&2, 6, 7, 9, 10, 13, 14), pull from Grind75's topic filter instead. The same source's [algorithm cheatsheets](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/algorithms/study-cheatsheet/) are the right pre-solve refresher when a topic's complexity table is needed fast.
## The Hard-Question Playlist — liquidslr Only, ~70 Problems
[[60_Claude/10_Source_Summaries/Github Ingestion/Jobs Starred/interview-company-wise-problems|liquidslr/leetcode-company-wise-problems]] (31.1k stars — confirmed 2026-10-04 that this and the vault note's older URL `interview-company-wise-problems` are the same repo, just renamed) is the **sole** hard-question source, chosen explicitly over the alternative candidate `snehasishroy/leetcode-companywise-interview-questions` — see Resources Considered and Rejected below. Pick a company relevant to the current internship search, filter that company's list to hard difficulty, and work down it by frequency where the CSV gives one. **Grind75 is confirmed to carry zero hard-tagged problems** — do not look there for hard questions; the entire ~70-problem semester target comes from liquidslr alone. This keeps the hard-problem quota anchored to real interview surface area instead of LeetCode's hard tag in the abstract, which skews toward puzzle-difficulty rather than interview-difficulty.
## Interview-Based Questions — the Other 2 a Day
The 2 daily non-LeetCode problems come from the same interview-prep resource base, used for question *style* rather than a raw problem count: [[60_Claude/10_Source_Summaries/Github Ingestion/Jobs Starred/coding-interview-university|Coding Interview University]]'s topic checklist (data structures, trees/graphs, complexity, sorting sections specifically — the OS/networking/compiler sections are out of scope here; confirmed 2026-10-04 still the best-regarded pick for this narrow use, nothing in Grokking the Coding Interview, InterviewBit, GfG's interview corner, or Exponent's guides beats it) supplies conceptual interview questions and mental-model prompts that don't reduce to a single LeetCode-style problem, and [[60_Claude/10_Source_Summaries/Github Ingestion/Jobs Starred/tech-interview-handbook|Tech Interview Handbook]]'s own non-Grind-75 material (behavioral questions, algorithm cheatsheet self-checks) fills the same role when the day's topic calls for it. [[20_Progress/Degree/_Courses/Technical Interview/CSCI 4041/Problems/Weekly Set|Weekly Set]] names the specific 2 for that week rather than leaving the choice open every day.
## Assessment / OA Practice
TIP103 grades on HackerRank directly, so that stays the primary assessment platform. Confirmed 2026-10-04, three real supplements, in priority order: **HackerRank's own [Interview Preparation Kit](https://www.hackerrank.com/interview/preparation-kits)** (topic-organized, company-tag filterable — free, official, complementary to the course's own HackerRank units); [perixtar/Tech-OA-Interview-Questions](https://github.com/perixtar/Tech-OA-Interview-Questions) (5.4k stars, daily-updated, tied to fastprep.io — the most current option for past-OA-style question sets specifically; not yet ingested as a full source summary, worth one in a future pass) when a specific assessment's format/level calls for it; and the **official company pages** where they exist — Amazon's own [SDE OA prep page](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/university/sde-oa) (unscored practice test, 2 sample questions, Amazon-published) and Google's [Tech Dev Guide](https://github.com/alvinctk/google-tech-dev-guide) (Google-engineer-authored practice material). Meta, Microsoft, and Apple have no equivalent official page as of this check — third-party sites fill that gap but aren't cited here since none were confirmed better than what's already in this stack.
## Resources Considered and Rejected
Documented so this doesn't get re-litigated. Easy/medium: Blind 75 (superseded by Grind75 per Grind75's own author), full NeetCode 150, Grind 169, AlgoMonster, and Sean Prashad's pattern list are all philosophically identical to Grind75 — none demonstrated as better for a 15-week plan. Hard: `snehasishroy/leetcode-companywise-interview-questions` (fresher snapshot, Java-only solutions) and AlgoExpert / Elements of Programming Interviews (broader paid courses, not frequency-ranked hard lists) were both passed over in favor of liquidslr. From `DSA Common To Share All`: only `DSA Imp 170 Questions - Leetcode.pdf` was pattern-and-difficulty-tagged (genuinely useful), and it's still dropped per explicit instruction — the whole folder is out of this build. Everything else in that folder (problem-title-only checklists, pre-written solution dumps, a stale 2022 Amazon snapshot, a pure complexity cheatsheet) was redundant or unusable as a problem source.
## Deprioritized For Now

Revisit only if a specific application explicitly asks for it.
