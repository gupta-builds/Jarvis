## 0:26 Internship and the CPT problem (10 min)
**Frame:** I am not asking him to fix this. I want his judgment and, if he has one, an introduction.
### Say it in four sentences
> "Here's the constraint. I'm an international student, and UMN paused CPT in August after new federal guidance. CPT was how most students did a summer internship. There's a federal court hearing on November 6, and nobody, including our international office, knows what happens."

> "So I'm planning for three routes at once: research work on campus, an internship that physically happens abroad, and pre-completion OPT, which I'd decide on around March. I'm still applying everywhere in the meantime."

Do not say more than this unless he asks. Facts and dates: [[Junior Year Internship Decision]], [[CPT Program]], [[ISSS October Meeting Brief]].
### Ask him (pick two)
1. "Have you seen international interns or new grads handled well at your company or others? What did the company actually do?"
2. "If my summer ends up as research or a serious project instead of an internship, how do I make that read as strong to a hiring manager?"
3. "Would a later start date, like July, be realistic for a company like yours?"
4. "How would you weigh a smaller company that can work around this against a big name that can't?"
### The one ask
> "Is there anyone in your network who has hired international students that I could talk to for fifteen minutes?"

He is not expected to provide jobs. The ask is advice or an introduction.
### Do not
- Say CPT is "closed forever" or that the court will fix it. Nov 6 is a hearing, not a decision, and not the university's.
- Call a personal or volunteer project a job. ISSS warned this can hurt OPT eligibility.
- Accept or imply any start date before the work authorization exists in writing.
