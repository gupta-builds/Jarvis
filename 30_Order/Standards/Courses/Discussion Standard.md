# Discussion Standard
==A discussion note preserves the reasoning that changed during the conversation: claims, evidence, objections, examples, and decisions—not a transcript of who spoke.==
This standard governs `Discussion Template.md`. Discussion notes are especially important for reading-based courses and writing-intensive classes: they capture the interpretation and questions that a lecture or textbook chapter may not expose.
## Frontmatter and provenance
Keep `type: class`, `input_kind: discussion`, `status`, dates, course `area`, `tags`, and `next`. Link the course Board, the matching week, reading, chapter, assignment, or writing task. Record the discussion date and section when known. `next` should name a concrete follow-up such as resolving a disputed interpretation or revising a paragraph.
Before writing, identify the assigned reading/questions and the source of the discussion material: personal notes, permitted transcript, handout, or instructor clarification. Do not present an agent's reconstruction as something said in the room.
## Pre-work
State what had to be read or prepared and what the source actually asked. Link the reading and list the questions that were live before discussion. If pre-work was not available, say so; do not invent a preparation task from the topic.
## Notes
Organize by claim or question, not by minute. For each meaningful point capture:
- the claim or interpretation;
- the evidence or example supporting it;
- an objection, alternative reading, or limitation;
- the correction or resolution, if one occurred;
- the consequence for the course concept, assignment, or paper.
Use speaker attribution only when it matters and is verified. Separate `Instructor clarification`, `Student interpretation`, and `Unresolved` when their authority differs. Quote sparingly; paraphrase and preserve the reasoning.
## Action items
Use checkboxes only for real work created by the discussion: revise a thesis, verify a citation, read a section, ask a question, update a concept note, or link a discovered example. Each task needs a done condition. Once complete, check it and record the resulting change in the relevant note.
## Course-note propagation
After discussion, update the affected concept, weekly synthesis, writing/homework note, or exam sheet. Do not duplicate the entire discussion into every note; carry forward only the durable claim, correction, example, or unresolved question, with a link back here.
## Integrity and uncertainty
Never invent attendance, participation, consensus, or a professor's position. If notes conflict with the reading or lecture, preserve the conflict and mark what must be checked. Discussion evidence can clarify emphasis, but it cannot silently override a textbook definition or published policy.
## Done conditions
- Pre-work is linked and its actual task is clear.
- Notes contain reasoning, evidence, objections, and corrections rather than a transcript.
- Unresolved questions are explicit and actionable.
- Action items have concrete done conditions.
- Durable changes were propagated to the correct course notes.
