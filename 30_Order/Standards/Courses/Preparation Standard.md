# Course Preparation Standard
==A preparation note converts the course's grading structure and schedule into recurring behaviors with a trigger, a time budget, and a visible definition of done.==
This standard governs the course preparation/operating note. It is not a motivational essay and it must not duplicate the full Board schedule. The Board records verified course facts; Preparation explains how those facts change the student's weekly behavior.
## Frontmatter
Keep `type: class`, `input_kind: preparation`, `status`, dates, the course Board in `area`, tags, and `next`. Link every high-leverage claim to the Board or an assessment note. Mark estimated hours as estimates and keep the assumptions visible.
## Grading Leverage
Rank assessments by verified weight, frequency, difficulty, and failure cost. Show the arithmetic that makes the priority meaningful. Include hidden deadlines such as code reviews, discussion participation, or note limits. For CSCI 4511W, preserve the important distinction between open-resource/open-collaboration short quizzes and individual, 45-minute long quizzes with a two-page note limit; never generalize one assessment's rules to another.
## Time Budget
Create a realistic recurring weekly rhythm from the actual meeting and due-date schedule. Separate pre-lecture reading, live lecture, discussion, assignment work, textbook note production, concept preparation, review, flashcards, and buffer time. Label any credit-hour or hour-per-week assumption. Before a major quiz/exam, show the backward plan rather than saying "study more."
## Must-Do Habits
Every habit needs a trigger and done condition. Strong examples:
- Before each lecture: read the assigned source, create/update the textbook note, and prepare concept scaffolds for the scheduled topic.
- During lecture: write live capture in the weekly note's `## Lecture` section; do not postpone capture for later polishing.
- After lecture: reconcile lecture capture with textbook, slides, code/PDFs, assignments, and concept notes; leave no unresolved conflict implicit.
- After every submission: record evidence, feedback, and a reusable failure pattern.
- Before an exam: build rich concept notes, then drill flashcards and solve planned questions rather than rereading passively.
## Common Mistakes / Traps
Record course-specific traps sourced from the Board, assignment prompts, lecture corrections, and grading feedback. Include rule collisions, missing secondary deadlines, likely misreadings, and the difference between preparation work and submission work. Never populate this section with generic advice unless the course source explicitly supports it.
## Professor-Specific Notes
Capture stated preferences and grading patterns with source/date. Separate `Verified`, `Observed repeatedly`, `Inferred`, and `Ask/confirm`. Do not infer a professor's preference from one ambiguous comment or from a different class.
## Maintenance cadence
Review this note at course start, after the first graded artifact, before each major assessment, and after meaningful policy/schedule changes. Update the habit system when a failure recurs; do not rewrite it for every ordinary lecture.
## Done conditions
- Priority follows verified grading leverage, not intuition.
- Time budgets include pre-lecture, lecture capture, post-lecture synthesis, assignments, and review.
- Habits have triggers and done conditions.
- Course-specific traps and professor-specific facts are sourced and labeled.
- The note produces observable action, not generic encouragement.
